        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>Dette kursus introducerer de centrale idéer og værktøjer, der anvendes til at studere kvante-nonlokalitet gennem sproget fra spil, Bell-uligheder og korrelationer. Vi vil kort gennemgå den kvantemekaniske formalisme, der er nødvendig for at beskrive delte systemer, målinger og strategier, før vi udvikler sondringen mellem klassiske, kvantemekaniske og mere generelle non-signalling-strategier. Undervejs vil vi møde vigtige familier af eksempler, herunder spil med algebraisk og grafteoretisk struktur, samt forbindelser til kvanteteoriens historiske grundlag. Et væsentligt fokus vil være på grafteoretiske spil, herunder graf-farvnings-, homomorfi- og isomorfispil, samt deres forbindelser til kvantegrafparametre og kvanteautomorfigrupper. Kurset afsluttes med en introduktion til self-testing, det device-independent paradigme og optiske implementeringer af device-independent protokoller.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Forelæsninger, øvelser, gruppearbejde</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>Formålet med dette kursus er at give et moderne perspektiv på nonlokalitet og hvordan dette studeres i aktuel forskning inden for fysik, matematik og datalogi, herunder praktiske anvendelser i kvanteteknologier såsom sikker generering af tilfældighed og certificering af kvanteenheder.</Course_Objectives>
        </Txt>
        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>This course introduces the core ideas and tools used to study quantum nonlocality through the language of games, Bell inequalities, and correlations. We will briefly review the quantum formalism needed to describe shared systems, measurements, and strategies, before developing the distinction between classical, quantum, and more general non-signalling strategies. Along the way, we will encounter important families of examples, including games with algebraic and graph-theoretic structure, as well as connections to the historical foundations of quantum theory. A substantial focus will be placed on graph-theoretic games, including graph colouring, homomorphism, and isomorphism games, and on their links to quantum graph parameters and quantum automorphism groups. The course will conclude by introducing self-testing, the device-independent paradigm, and optical implementations of device-independent protocols.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Lectures, exercises, group work</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>The aim of this course is to provide a modern perspective on nonlocality and how it is studied in current research across physics, mathematics, and computer science, including practical applications in quantum technologies such as secure randomness generation and the certification of quantum devices.</Course_Objectives>
