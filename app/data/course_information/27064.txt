          <Course_Objectives>Introduktion til heterolog proteinproduktion fra gendesign til oprensning og karakterisering. Kurset integrerer molekylærbiologi, værtselektion, celle engineering og bioprocesprincipper, der er relevante for bioteknologiske og farmaceutiske applikationer.

Kurset gør de studerende i stand til at analysere og designe strategier for proteinproduktion, som er relevante for farmaceutiske produkter, industrielle enzymer og bæredygtige biosolutions‑applikationer.</Course_Objectives>

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>The course covers the principles of heterologous protein production including: gene design and vector construction; promoter and ribosome binding site selection; host systems such as bacteria, yeast, mammalian, insect, plant and cell-free platforms; cellular engineering strategies; upstream bioprocess development and scale-up principles; downstream processing including affinity, ion exchange and size exclusion chromatography; and analytical techniques for protein characterization.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Lectures combined with theoretical and case-based exercises. Students work throughout the semester on a recurring protein case study</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Literature>Scientific papers and other text (to be provided by lecturers)</Course_Literature>
          <Course_Objectives>Introduction to heterologous protein production from gene design to purification and characterization. The course integrates molecular biology, host selection, cell engineering, and bioprocess principles relevant for biotechnology and pharmaceutical applications.
The course provides students with a comprehensive understanding of recombinant protein production across molecular, cellular, and process scales. Students gain insight into how genetic design, host selection, cell engineering, and downstream processing are integrated in modern biotechnology. The course enables students to analyze and design protein production strategies relevant to pharmaceuticals, industrial enzyme, and sustainable biosolution applications.</Course_Objectives>
