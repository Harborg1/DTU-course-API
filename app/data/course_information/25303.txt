        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>Kurset introducerer matematisk modellering af populationsdynamik hos levende organismer, fra bakterier til hvaler. Fokus er på konceptuelle modeller baseret på differentialligninger, der illustrerer grundlæggende begreber i populationsmodellering. Det overordnede mål er at konstruere nye matematiske modeller af en konkret biologisk virkelighed, baseret på eksisterende simple modeller. På denne måde introducerer kurset både teknikken til at udvikle dine egne modeller; denne færdighed er generel og ikke specifik for biologiske systemer. De vigtigste emner er: enkeltarts-populationsmodeller (logistisk vækst og strukturerede populationer). Modeller af interagerende populationer (konkurrence og rovdyr-byttedyr relationer). Funktionelle responser. Somatisk vækstmodellering. Udnyttelse af levende systemer. Sæsonmæssig succession. Epidemiologiske modeller. Rumlig dynamik. Gennem hele kurset lægges der vægt på at udvikle præsentationsevner for matematiske modeller. Ved slutningen af kurset udvikler de studerende deres eget projekt, som vil blive evalueret ved en plakat-session.

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>The course introduces mathematical modeling of population dynamics of living organisms, from bacteria to whales. Focus is on conceptual models based on differential equations that illustrate basic concepts in population modelling. The overarching goal is to construct new mathematical models of a concrete biological reality, based upon existing simple models. In this manner the course both introduces to the technique of developing your own models; this skill is general and not specific for biological systems. The main topics are: single-species population models (logistic growth and structured populations). Models of interacting populations (competition and predator-prey relations). Functional responses. Somatic growth modelling. Exploitation of living systems. Seasonal succession. Epidemiological models. Spatial dynamics. Throughout, there is emphasis on developing presentation skills of mathematical models. At the end of the course the students develop their own project which will be evaluated by a poster session.

Course Literature
Karline Soetaert and Peter M.J. Herman: A practical guide to ecological modelling (freely available for DTU students).</Contents>

          <Course_Objectives>The course is an introduction to mathematical modelling of biological systems. It will enable the student to evaluate, develop and apply simple dynamical population models to living systems, both in industrial settings (bioreactors or aquaculture) or in free-living natural populations.
The course is for any master students or 5th semester bachelor students – no prior biological knowledge is required.</Course_Objectives>
