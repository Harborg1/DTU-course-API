        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>Kurset introducerer teoretisk og eksperimentelt molekylær termodynamik fra et kemisk, fysisk, og biologisk perspektiv.

I den teoretiske del af kurset vil vi undersøge både passive og aktive transportmekanismer, dvs. diffusion og advektion, så vel som vigtige aspekter af reaktions-ligevægt og -kinetik, inklusive Gibbs fri energi, ligevægtskonstanter, og indflydelsen af solvatisering. Vi vil sammenkæde disse fænomener med biologiske og biologiske processer i biomolekylære analysesystemer og i levende mammale cellekulturer. Vi vil endvidere anvende den kombinerede teoretiske indsigt til at udføre prædiktive numeriske simuleringer af transport, ligevægt, og forbrug af stoffer i et biomolekylært system. 

I den eksperimentelle del vil vi anvende den opnåede viden på udvalgte eksperimentelle systemer, som for eksempel assays for bindingsaffinitet, biomolekylære assays, og levende cellekulturer. Et essentielt aspekt af denne del vil være eksperimentel træning i vigtige procedurer til dyrkning af mammale cellekulturer og nøglemetoder til at beskrive overlevelse, metabolisme og deling af de dyrkede celler.</Contents>

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>The course introduces theoretical and experimental molecular thermodynamics from a chemical, physical, and biological perspective.
 
In the theoretical part of the course, we will explore both passive and active transport mechanisms, i.e., diffusion and advection, as well as core aspects of reaction equilibrium and kinetics, including Gibbs free energy, equilibrium constants, and solvation effects. We will link these phenomena to biological and bioanalytical processes in biomolecular assays and in live mammalian cell cultures. We will further apply the combined theoretical insight to perform predictive numerical simulation of the transport, equilibrium, and consumption of compounds in a biomolecular system.

In the experimental part, we will apply the acquired knowledge on selected experimental systems, such as binding affinity assays, biomolecular assays, and live cell cultures. An essential aspect of this part will be experimental training in core mammalian cell culture procedures and key methods to characterize cell viability, cell metabolism, and cell proliferation.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Lectures, problem sessions, classroom discussions, text book exercises, experimental exercises.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Literature>Molecular Driving Forces (Statistical Thermodynamics in Biology, Chemistry, Physics and Nanoscience), Second Edition, Ken. A. Dill and Sarina Bromberg, Garland Science. ISBN 978-0-8153- 4430-8. 

Howard Berg: Random Walks in Biology, 1993, 152 p., Princeton University Press, ISBN 0691000646

Handouts will be provided during the course and will be part of the course curriculum.</Course_Literature>
          <Course_Objectives>The objective of the course is to provide theoretical insight into thermodynamics and transport processes at the length scale of individual biomolecules and other macromolecules, and to experimentally apply the obtained insight on live mammalian cell cultures and biomolecular analysis systems. The aim is to give students a joint theoretical and experimental understanding of the forces and phenomena that drive molecular interactions in biological and biochemical systems and devices at the nanometer to millimeter scale.</Course_Objectives>
