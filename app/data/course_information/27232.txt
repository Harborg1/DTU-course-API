          <Contents>Kurset omhandler konstruktion og anvendelse af matematiske modeller af cellulær metabolisme baseret på stoikiometriske og constraint-baserede tilgange. Hovedfokus er på prokaryote organismer, men kurset vil også omfatte eksempler og hjemmeopgaver baseret på eukaryote og mammale systemer for at illustrere den brede anvendelighed og de mange praktiske anvendelser inden for metabolisk systembiologi.

Kurset består af forelæsninger, der introducerer centrale begreber, kombineret med guidede øvelser i undervisningen, som understøtter og udbygger forelæsningsstoffet. Derudover vil de studerende arbejde med opgaver og et gruppeprojekt. Kursusmaterialet omfatter en lærebog, videnskabelige artikler, multi-omics-datasæt fra offentlige databaser, gæsteforelæsninger og supplerende videoer. Alt beregningsarbejde udføres i Python.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Kurset kombinerer forelæsninger, opgavesæt, case-studier fra litteraturen og projektarbejde for at understøtte både teoretisk forståelse og praktisk anvendelse.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Literature>Systems Biology: Constraint-based Reconstruction and Analysis. Forfatter: Bernhard Palsson. Cambridge University Press. 2015</Course_Literature>
          <Course_Objectives>Kurset introducerer de studerende til teori og praksis indenfor opbygning af matematiske modeller af cellulær metabolisme med anvendelser i både prokaryote og mammale systemer. De centrale modelleringsrammer omfatter stoikiometriske og constraint-baserede tilgange implementeret i Python. Der er særligt fokus på de diverse anvendelsesmuligheder af modellerne. Gennem eksempler og øvelser vil de studerende opbygge og anvende constraint-baserede modeller til at adressere bioteknologiske problemstillinger såsom design af mikrobielle og mammale produktionsstammer, analyse af lægemidlers effekt på metaboliske pathways, optimering af mediesammensætning samt undersøgelse af interaktioner i mikrobielle samfund. Kurset omfatter desuden integration af transkriptom-, proteom- og metabolomdata i metaboliske modeller. Ved kursets afslutning vil de studerende kunne konstruere og anvende metaboliske modeller samt kritisk vurdere, hvilke biologiske spørgsmål modellerne kan og ikke kan besvare.</Course_Objectives>

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>This course covers the construction and application of mathematical models of cellular metabolism based on stoichiometric and constraints-based approaches. The primary focus is on prokaryotic organisms, but the course will also include examples and homework assignments based on eukaryotic and mammalian systems to highlight the generality and the diverse practical applications of metabolic systems biology.

The course will comprise lectures that introduce core concepts along with guided in‑class assignments that reinforce and extend the lecture material. Students will also complete homework assignments and a group project. The course material will include a textbook, scientific articles, multi‑omic datasets from public repositories, guest lectures, and supplementary videos. All computational work will be carried out in Python.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>The course combines lectures, problem sets, case studies from the literature, and project work to support both theoretical understanding and practical application.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Literature>Systems Biology: Constraint-based Reconstruction and Analysis. Author: Bernhard Palsson. Cambridge University Press. 2015</Course_Literature>
          <Course_Objectives>This course introduces students to the theory and practice of building mathematical models of cellular metabolism, with applications spanning both prokaryotic and mammalian systems. The principal modeling frameworks covered are stoichiometric and constraint-based approaches, implemented in Python. A central aim is to illustrate the diverse ways in which the models can be used. Through numerous examples and exercises, students will build and apply constraint‑based models to a range of biotechnological problems, such as microbial and mammalian strain design, analyzing drug effects on metabolic pathways, optimizing media formulations, and exploring microbial community interactions. The course also covers strategies for incorporating transcriptomic, proteomic, and metabolomic datasets into metabolic models. By the end of the course, students will be able to construct and apply metabolic models, and will have developed the critical judgment to understand what biological questions these models can and cannot address.</Course_Objectives>
