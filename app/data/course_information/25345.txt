        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>Statistics is a very broad discipline, but the part of statistics that will be the subject of this introductory course is practical statistical data analysis. 

The goal is to present a coherent common-sense approach to drawing conclusions from observing nature.

The approach should end up producing 1) correct estimates of quantities of interest, 2) correct quantification of uncertainties, and 3) correct conclusions w.r.t. relevant hypothesis. 
That’s it.

It may sound simple to interpret measured information, and sometimes it is — if measurements are originating from a tightly controlled lab experiment, but the very process of measuring nature may present challenges that needs to be accounted for. The challenges can be caused by what is practically possible. It may be impossible to obtain measurements which are representative (e.g. if part of the area or population is inaccessible), or it may be impossible to directly measure the quantity of interest (e.g. the number of fish alive), but possible to measure some related quantity (e.g. number of caught fish). The challenges can also be caused by non-linear behavior of the system beeing measured. 

This course gives an introduction to the basic steps that needs to be undertaken. The theory is demonstrated, examplified, and practiced via examples taken mainly from natural resource management.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Lectures, exercises (in class +
minature written), and 2 written reports.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Literature>Lecture notes and textbook sections (e.g. Pawitan: In all likelihood or Millar: Maximum likelihood estimation and inference: with examples in R, SAS and ADMB or Bolker: Ecological Models and Data in R)</Course_Literature>
          <Course_Objectives>The course aims to give students a basic general understanding of statistical models. The context is the general scientific method of confronting theoretical models  with measured observations. 
Important statistical principals are explained and exemplified among those are:
Data collection, estimation of model parameters, model selection, model validation, and quantification of uncertainties.
Examples are selected to match model types often seen in natural resource management.</Course_Objectives>
