        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>This course introduces sustainability and life cycle thinking in the construction sector with a focus on the application of life cycle assessment (LCA) to buildings in accordance with current Danish building regulations. Students learn key LCA concepts - definition of goals and scope, Life Cycle Inventory, Life Cycle Impact Assessment, interpretation and reporting and gain practical experience through the use of LCA tools (e.g. LCAbyg) and relevant data sources (generic databases and environmental product declarations). The teaching combines lectures with structured group work. During the course, guest lectures from industry will connect the various course themes (assessment practice, sustainable design, circular construction) with real engineering and architectural workflows. In parallel, groups work on the implementation of a semester project where students will carry out LCA studies on case buildings, where the design is subsequently optimized by implementing environmental impact reduction measures. During the course, students will have the opportunity to receive feedback and guidance during weekly group work as well as through mandatory peer review sessions.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Lectures, exercises and project work</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>The course provides students with a basic understanding of sustainability and life cycle assessment (LCA) in construction. A central goal is that students can perform and communicate a comprehensive LCA for a building in accordance with current industry practice. Through guided use of LCA tools (e.g. LCAbyg), both generic databases and environmental product declarations (EPDs) are used. Project-based case studies teach students to identify environmental impacts in buildings and provide them with knowledge that can justify design changes aimed at reducing climate impacts or resource consumption. This builds practical competencies for sustainability-oriented decision-making, including an understanding of how regulatory requirements, data quality and methodological choices affect previous results. Overall, students gain industry-relevant skills in modeling buildings in LCA software, working critically with EPD and database input, interpreting results to identify hotspots and proposing optimization and circular strategies. After completing the course, students will have acquired competencies that can be transferred directly to project-based work in engineering practice and gain a basic understanding of sustainability that can be applied in later courses on materials, structures, energy consumption and building design.</Course_Objectives>
