        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>I den første del af kurset introduceres det teoretiske grundlag for ekstrusions-baseret og lys-baseret 3D-print. Vi gennemgår de kemiske og fysiske principper samt kompatibilitet med levende celler.

Kursets anden del vil give den studerende praktisk erfaring med de centrale trin i lysbaseret og ekstrusions-baseret 3D-print, via strukturerede øvelser.

I den tredje og sidste del af kurset vil de studerende formulere deres egen 3D-print materialer og udarbejde printkoder med henblik på at genskabe en specifik vævsstruktur.

NB: Grundet kursets praktiske karakter, er deltagelse i øvelsesgange er obligatorisk</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>4 timer om ugen. Problemløsning, forelæsninger, klassediskussioner, laboratorieøvelser og projektarbejde</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Literature>Litteratur til kurset vil blive udleveret af underviserne</Course_Literature>
          <Course_Objectives>3D-print givernye muligheder inden for biomedicinsk ingeniørarbejde og forskning. Additive fremstillingsmetoder er unikt egnede til at formgive bløde, vandrige og dynamiske materialer, der minder om menneskelig væv. Derudover gør 3D print med levende menneskeceller - kendt som 3D-bioprintning - det muligt direkte at fremstille biologiske væv.    3D print åbner således nye muligheder inden for biomedicinsk ingeniørarbejde, fra præklinisk forskning og stamcellebaserede terapier til avancerede kirurgiske anvendelser.

Dette kursus vil introducere dig til de grundlæggende principper for nøglemetoder inden for 3D-printning til biomedicinsk ingeniørarbejde med fokus på blødt og levende materiale. Du vil få praktisk erfaring med udvalgte 3D-printmetoder og lære styrker og svagheder ved:

· Ekstrusionsbaseret (bio)-printning, herunder direct-ink-write og embedded printing

· Lysbaseret printning, herunder stereolitografi og to-foton polymerisation</Course_Objectives>

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>In the first part of the course, we will introduce the theoretical foundation for extrusion-based and light-based 3D printing. This will cover chemical and physical principles, as well as compatibility with living cells.

The second part will provide hands-on experience with key steps and 3D printing using light-based and extrusion-based 3D printing, based on structured exercises.

In the third and last part of the course, students will formulate their own 3D printing ink and print codes toward replicating a specific tissue architecture.

NB: Due to the practical nature of the course, participation in exercise sessions is mandatory</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>4 hours per week. Problem-based activities, lectures, classroom discussions, lab exercises and project work</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Literature>Readings will be provided by lectures</Course_Literature>
          <Course_Objectives>3D printing technologies are opening new possibilities within biomedical engineering and research. Additive manufacturing methods are particularly well suited for shaping soft, water-rich, and dynamic materials that resemble human tissues. In addition, printing with living human cells - known as 3D bioprinting - enables the direct fabrication of engineered biological tissues. Together, these approaches are becoming integral across numerous areas of biomedical engineering, from preclinical research and stem cell–based therapies to advanced surgical applications.

This course will introduce you to the fundamental principles of key 3D printing methods for biomedical engineering, focusing on soft and living matter. You will gain hands-on experience with selected 3D printing methods, and learn the strengths and weaknesses of:

· Extrusion-based (bio)-printing, including direct-ink-write and embedded printing

· Light-based printing, including stereolithography and two-photon polymerization</Course_Objectives>
