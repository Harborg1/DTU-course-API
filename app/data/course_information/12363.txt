          <Contents>Dynamiske bygningssimuleringsværktøjer spiller en central rolle i analyse og forudsigelse af bygningers ydeevne under varierende forhold. Dette kursus fokuserer på at anvende både standard og avancerede funktioner i IDA ICE til at undersøge indeklima, energiforbrug og systemadfærd. Kurset giver deltagerne omfattende praktisk erfaring med opsætning af simuleringer og arbejdet med den underliggende modelleringsstruktur i IDA ICE.
De studerende introduceres til den matematiske modelleringsmetode i IDA ICE, herunder zonedeling, controllere og opbygningen af interne modelkomponenter. De lærer at implementere makroer og avancerede styringsstrategier til at repræsentere belægningsmønstre og brugeradfærd, samt hvordan disse påvirker indeklima og energiforbrug.
Derudover gør kurset deltagerne i stand til at anvende automatiske parametervariationer og optimeringsværktøjer i IDA ICE. De får erfaring med at generere og håndtere outputfiler, logge relevante variable og bruge programmets visualiseringsfunktioner til at fortolke simuleringsresultater. Dette omfatter evaluering af tidsserier, aggregerede resultater og andre outputformater, der er relevante for vurdering af designs.
Gennem arbejdet med disse elementer udvikler de studerende evnen til kritisk at analysere simuleringsresultater og vurdere både muligheder og begrænsninger ved avanceret bygningsperformance-modellering.</Contents>

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>Dynamic building simulation tools play a central role in analysing and predicting how buildings perform under varying conditions. This course focuses on applying both standard and advanced functionalities of IDA ICE to investigate indoor environment, energy use and system behaviour. The course provides participants with extensive hands on experience in setting up simulations and working with the underlying modelling framework of IDA ICE.
The students are introduced to the mathematical modelling approach in IDA ICE, including zone division, controllers, and the structure of internal model components. They learn how to implement macros and advanced control strategies to represent occupancy patterns and occupant behaviour, and how these influence indoor climate and energy performance.
Furthermore, the course enables participants to apply automated parameter variations and optimisation tools within IDA ICE. They gain experience in generating and managing output files, logging relevant variables, and using the program’s result visualisation features to interpret simulation outcomes. This includes evaluating time series, aggregated results and other output formats relevant for performance assessment.
By working through these elements, the students develop the ability to critically analyse simulation results and assess both the possibilities and limitations of advanced building performance modelling.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Group work, exercises, student presentations and lectures</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>The course introduces students to the simulation of indoor environment and energy use in buildings and systems using IDA ICE. Participants learn how to design system, assign them to a building geometry, and specify customized control strategies for both active and passive systems.
Students explore the modelling principles behind building energy simulation tools, gaining insight into how IDA ICE structures its mathematical models, zoning, and system controls.
The course emphasizes understanding of stochastic models for occupancy and occupant behaviour, with particular focus on implementation of such models and how they influence simulation results. Students learn to assess when these models are appropriate and how they can be applied in performance evaluations.
By the end of the course, participants are equipped to use advanced tools in IDA ICE to set up and run simulations and, enabling them to critically evaluate modelling assumptions and simulation outputs in a building performance context.</Course_Objectives>
          <Remark>The course targets engineering students at master level that are interested in the use of advanced, state-of-the-art methodologies and programs to simulate the consequences for indoor environment and building energy consumption of decisions made at the design stage. However, the course may also be followed by PhD students with the required basic knowledge that are interested in learning how to use the advanced levels of IDA ICE.

The course relies on students bringing their own PC. The programs used in the course requires a Windows based PC.</Remark>
