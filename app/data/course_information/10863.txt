        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>This course is primarily meant as an experimental course, where the students have the chance to use state-of-the-art fabrication and characterization technology. 25% of the course hours are dedicated to theory lectures, guided experimental planning, and data analysis, while the remaining 75% are dedicated to independent work. Besides specific technical skills within microscale 3D and 4D printing, this course supports the development of transferrable skills related to the planning and performing of experiments, and to disseminating research results.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Autonomous experimental (group) work with an introduction to equipment and theory.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Literature>https://doi.org/10.3390/micro1020013 , https://doi.org/10.1016/j.addma.2023.103761 , https://doi.org/10.1002/admt.202401950</Course_Literature>
          <Course_Objectives>The goal of this course is to provide you with hands-on experience in the design, fabrication and characterization of responsive polymer microstructures. You will be introduced to the highest resolution technology for 3D printing, which is two-photon polymerization (2PP). You will also learn about 4D printing, which employs smart materials to fabricate freeform structures that respond to changes in their environment. You will learn to plan and carry out experiments in advanced laboratories, with focus on safe working procedures. You will propose your own designs for target applications of micro printing within microrobotics, drug delivery, microscale sensing, or cell culture. Finally, you will document your results and present them together with a theory background and relevant literature.</Course_Objectives>
