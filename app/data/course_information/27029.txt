        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>Kurset introducerer de studerende til laboratorieautomation inden for biovidenskab med et stærkt fokus på praktisk implementering og brugen af pipetteringsrobotplatforme. Undervisningen er struktureret som et integreret forløb bestående af forelæsninger, case-baseret arbejde, laboratorieimplementering og plenumrefleksion.
Forelæsningerne (videoer) introducerer det teoretiske grundlag for laboratorieautomation, herunder de grundlæggende principer for Opentrons OT-2-platformen, væskehåndtering (herunder væskeklasser), standardisering af arbejdsgange og grundlæggende Python-programmering til styring af robotter.
Efter den teoretiske introduktion præsenteres de studerende for en konkret case (f.eks. serielle fortyndinger, PCR, ELISA, DNA-oprensning eller enzymanalyser). I mindre grupper analyserer de den eksperimentelle problemstilling og udvikler en teoretisk automatiseringstrategi, der oversætter en manuel laboratorieprotokol til et struktureret robotworkflow med fokus på begrænsninger, fleksibilitet og optimering.
Efterfølgende implementerer og tester de studerende deres teoretiske løsninger i laboratoriet på OT-2-platformen. Denne praktiske fase giver de studerende erfaring med både robottekniske og biologiske udfordringer, der skal overkommes for effektivt at automatisere eksperimentelle arbejdsgange. Hvert modul afsluttes med en fælles opsamling i plenum, hvor resultater, udfordringer og designvalg diskuteres, og de centrale læringspointer sammenfattes.
Kurset afsluttes med et eksamensprojekt, hvor studerende i mindre grupper, designer, implementerer og tester et nyt automatiseret workflow til OT-2-platformen, der integrerer de teoretiske og praktiske kompetencer, der er udviklet gennem hele kurset.</Contents>

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>The course introduces students to laboratory automation in the life sciences, with a strong emphasis on practical implementation and the use of pipetting robotics platforms. The teaching is structured as an integrated sequence of lectures, case-based work, laboratory implementation, and plenary reflection.
The video lectures introduce the theoretical foundation of laboratory automation, including the operating principles of the Opentrons OT-2 platform, liquid-handling concepts (e.g., liquid classes), workflow standardization, and the fundamentals of Python programming for robotic control.
Following the theoretical introduction, students are presented with a concrete case (e.g., serial dilutions, PCR, ELISA, DNA purification, or enzyme assays). Working in small groups, they analyze the experimental objective and develop a theoretical automation strategy, e.g., translating a manual laboratory protocol into a structured robotic workflow while considering constraints, flexibility, and optimization.
The proposed solutions are then implemented and tested in the laboratory using the OT-2 platform. This hands-on phase allows students to confront both the technical and biological challenges of automating experimental workflows. Each module concludes with a plenary session in which results, challenges, and design choices are discussed, and key learning points are synthesized.
The course culminates in an exam project in which students, working in small teams, design, implement, and test a new automated workflow for the OT-2 platform, integrating the theoretical and practical competencies developed throughout the course.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Lectures and exercises: 

Preparation: Read literature and watch video lectures that introduce the theoretical basis for automation, standardization, programming of robots, and design of experiments.

In class teaching: 
1. Introduction of case and design challenge.
2. Theoretical exercises: Design of experiments, incl. programming of OT-2 robot.
3. Laboratory exercises: Students implement, test and troubleshoot robot-based experiments.
4. Plenary summary regarding learnings.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Literature>Selected reviews and original articles, and the manual for the Opentron OT-2 pipetting robot.</Course_Literature>
          <Course_Objectives>The course introduces biotechnology students to the theoretical foundations of laboratory automation and equips them with the practical skills required to operate Opentron's OT-2 pipetting robot platform. Students will gain insight into how robotic systems can be used to perform biological laboratory experiments and how automation can improve reproducibility, increase sample throughput, and improve data quality. The course emphasizes interdisciplinary skills by integrating biotechnology, programming (Python), and systems thinking, ensuring that students can design, implement, and optimize automated experimental workflows. Upon completion of the course, students will be able to design, document, implement, and evaluate simple automated workflows that perform common life science experiments.</Course_Objectives>
