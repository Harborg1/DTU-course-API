          <Contents>Stamcellebaserede teknologier skaber nye muligheder inden for biomedicinsk forskning, lægemiddeludvikling og regenerativ medicin. Dette kursus giver ingeniørstuderende et solidt teoretisk grundlag inden for dette hurtigt udviklende felt og sætter fokus på samspillet mellem stamcellebiologi, differentieringsstrategier og anvendelser i sygdomsmodellering og terapi.
Gennem kurset introduceres de centrale principper for stamcellebiologi, herunder definitioner, potensniveauer, kilder til stamceller og inducerede pluripotente stamceller samt de etiske overvejelser, der knytter sig til deres anvendelse. Med udgangspunkt i disse grundelementer gennemgås metoder til at styre stamceller mod specifikke linjer via cytokiner, signalveje, små molekyler og ingeniørede kulturmiljøer. De studerende arbejder med organotypiske kulturer, organoider og centrale principper for at etablere in vitro sygdomsmodeller. Koncepter relateret til celleerstatningsterapi—som immunologisk matchning, immunsuppression og kvalitetskontrol (GMP)—introduceres sammen med genredigering og avancerede metoder til karakterisering af stamceller og deres derivater.
På tværs af kurset introduceres AI‑baserede værktøjer som nye forskningsredskaber. De studerende lærer, hvordan AI kan understøtte analyse, modeludvikling og teoretisk forsøgsdesign inden for stamcelleforskning.</Contents>

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>Stem cell–based technologies are transforming biomedical research, drug development, and regenerative medicine. This course provides engineering students with a solid theoretical foundation in this rapidly developing field, with emphasis on the interplay between stem cell biology, differentiation strategies, and applications in disease modelling and therapy.
Throughout the course, students are introduced to the core principles of stem cell biology, including definitions, potency states, sources of stem cells and induced pluripotent stem cells, and the ethical considerations associated with their use. Building on these fundamentals, the course examines how stem cells are guided toward specific lineages using cytokines, pathway modulation, small molecules, and engineered culture environments. Challenges such as culture stability, contaminating cell populations, purification approaches, automated culture systems, and slow‑release technologies are discussed in relation to practical decision‑making in differentiation design.
A substantial part of the course focuses on how stem cells are used to model human disease and support drug development. Students work with organotypic cultures, organoid systems, and key principles in creating in vitro disease models. Concepts related to cell replacement therapy—such as immunological matching, immunosuppression, and quality assurance (GMP)—are introduced together with gene‑editing strategies and analytical methods for characterizing stem cells and their derivatives.
Across the course, AI‑based tools are presented as emerging research instruments. Students learn how AI can support analysis, model development, and conceptual experimental design within stem‑cell–based research.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>We use a flipped classroom approach as the primary teaching format. This consists of short lectures followed by learning activities. AI is used throughout the course to develop conceptual models of methodological approaches.
Students submit a short assignment almost every week, such as a poster, sketch, video presentation, or a verified prompting strategy. All assignments must be submitted and approved before students can be admitted to the exam.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Literature>Lecture note, review articles, book chapters and generative AI.</Course_Literature>
          <Course_Objectives>The objective of this master-level course is to provide students with a comprehensive understanding of stem cell biology and its applications in regenerative medicine and the pharmaceutical industry. In this course students will delve into theoretical aspects of stem cell definitions, differentiation protocols, disease modeling, and therapeutic strategies. The course will also cover essential topics such as culture methods, gene editing, and regulatory requirements. By the end of the course, students will be equipped with the knowledge and analytical skills needed for advanced research and professional practice in the field of stem cell engineering. The aim is also other give the students training in using generative AI (LLM) as a research tool.</Course_Objectives>
