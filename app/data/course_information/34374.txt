        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>Kurset introducerer principper for IoT-hardware med fokus på grundlæggende koncepter, arkitekturer og praktiske hardwarehensyn. Der arbejdes med skematisk design og layout af printkort (PCB) ved brug af professionel CAD-software, Altium Designer. Kurset dækker essentielle aspekter af IoT-hardwareudvikling, herunder valg og integration af strømforsyningsmoduler (f.eks. LDO, buck-konvertere og batteristyrings-IC'er), STM32-mikrocontrollere samt trådløse kommunikationsmoduler (GPS og LoRa) med særligt fokus på RF-kredsdesign og impedanstilpasning. De studerende får praktisk erfaring med at forberede PCB-design til produktion, herunder udarbejdelse af nødvendig fremstillingsdokumentation (BOM, Gerber-filer).

De studerende arbejder praktisk med projektarbejde i grupper og fører et koncept gennem hele IoT-hardwareudviklingsprocessen. Praktiske øvelser og vejledninger understøtter projektforløbet. Evalueringen baseres på øvelser gennemført i løbet af kurset samt en afsluttende teknisk rapport, der dokumenterer hardwaredesignet og udviklingsprocessen.

De studerende forventes at have grundlæggende kendskab til elektroniske kredsløb. Der kræves ingen specifikke lærebøger; kursusmateriale stilles til rådighed.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Ugentlige forelæsninger og praktiske øvelser understøtter en projektbaseret tilgang, hvor studerende designer og dokumenterer en komplet IoT hardware prototype gennem individuelt og gruppearbejde.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>

Centrale emner inkluderer design af strømforsyningsarkitektur, integration af mikrocontrollere samt design af hardwaregrænseflader til GPS- og LoRa-moduler. De studerende genererer produktionsfiler og lærer praktiske arbejdsgange til udvikling af omkostningseffektive, lavenergi IoT-enheder, klar til prototyper og produktion.</Course_Objectives>

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>The course introduces IoT hardware principles, covering fundamental concepts, architectures, and practical hardware considerations. It includes schematic design and printed circuit board (PCB) layout using professional CAD software Altium Designer. The course addresses critical aspects of IoT hardware development, including selection and integration of power management modules (e.g., LDO, buck converters, and battery management ICs), STM32 microcontrollers, and wireless communication modules (GPS and LoRa), focusing particularly on RF circuit design and impedance matching. Students gain practical experience in preparing PCB designs for fabrication, including creating essential manufacturing documentation (BOM, Gerber files).

Students will engage in hands-on project work in groups, taking a concept through the entire IoT hardware development process. Practical exercises and tutorials are included to support project development. Evaluation is based on exercises completed during the course and a final technical report documenting the hardware design and development process.

Students are expected to have basic knowledge of electronic circuits. No specific textbooks are required; course materials will be provided.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Weekly lectures and practical exercises support a project-based approach, where students design and document a complete IoT hardware prototype through individual and group work.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>The course provides students with hands-on experience in designing, developing, and preparing IoT hardware systems for production. Through project-based learning, students follow the full hardware development process, from concept and schematic design to PCB layout, and component selection. Key topics include power supply architecture, microcontroller integration, and designing interfaces for GPS and LoRa modules. Students will generate manufacturing files and learn practical workflows for building cost-efficient, low-power IoT devices ready for prototyping and production.</Course_Objectives>
          <DTU_Remark>The course will not be offered in Autumn in any future years. It will always be scheduled in the Spring moving forward.</DTU_Remark>
          <Remark>This course focuses on IoT hardware design and complements other DTU IoT courses such as 34325, 34346, 34365, and 34373, which focus mainly on networking technologies, communication protocols, and embedded software development.</Remark>
