          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Forelæsning og øvelser</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>Kurset har til formål at give studerende en dybere forståelse af, hvordan menneskelig adfærd kan studeres empirisk. Kurset udforsker kortlægning af årsag-og-virkning-forhold gennem eksperimentelle metoder, analyser af naturligt forekommende data, valg-eksperimenter til måling af adfærd i relation til ikke-eksisterende eller komplekse goder samt kvalitativt feltarbejde. Et centralt tema i kurset er den kontinuerlige diskussion af den teoretiske forankring, passende valg af empiriske metoder og potentielle synergier mellem metoder. Vi diskuterer også etisk brug af metoderne.</Course_Objectives>

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>Section 1 - Experimental Economic Methods:
- Causality: What is causality, why is it important, and when does it make a difference?
- Experimental Design: How can experiments be designed to establish causality, and what  
  are the key principles behind a successful experiment?
- Types of Experiments: What are the different types of experiments, what are their 
  strengths and weaknesses, and when should one be used over another?
- Ethical consideration of economic experiments

Section 2 - Methods for naturally occurring data and ethics in research:
- Methods for policy makers: Analyzing and understanding natural experiment for policy analyses. Estimating and critically applying elasticities for policy analyses.
- Ethics in research: Ethical considerations in the design, analyses, and dissemination of research studies. 

Section 3 - Experimental analysis of hypothetical choices:
This section explores methods for eliciting people's preferences for non-market goods and services, with a particular focus on the experimental analysis of human decision-making in hypothetical choice situations. It explores how individuals' preferences are estimated and critically examines the ethical considerations that arise. Students will gain hands-on knowledge of stated preference methods, such as contingent valuation and choice experiments, as well as revealed preference methods.

Section 4 - Researching human diversity ’in the wild’:
- Ethnography (participant observation and in situ interviews)
- Material analytics (network analysis, object-oriented analysis)
- Valuation struggles and ethics</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Lectures and Exercises</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>This course aims to provide students with a deeper understanding of how human behavior can be studied empirically. It explores the mapping of cause-and-effect relationships through experimental methods, analyses of naturally occurring data, choice experiments for measuring behavior in relation to non-existing or complex goods, and qualitative fieldwork. A central theme of the course is the continuous discussion of the theoretical grounding, the appropriate choice of empirical method, and potential synergies between methods. We also discuss using the methods ethically.</Course_Objectives>
