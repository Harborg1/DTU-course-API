          <Course_Objectives>I en lineær økonomi omdannes ressourcer til produkter, og produkterne ender i sidste ende som affald. I en cirkulær økonomi genbruges produkter og materialer, og affald elimineres så meget som muligt. En cirkulær økonomi kombineret med vedvarende energi bør resultere i et mere bæredygtigt system. I en bæredygtighedskontekst har fermenteringsprocesser evnen til at omdanne vedvarende ressourcer til nyttige produkter. Derfor kan fermentering betragtes som en af ​​hjørnestenene i opbygning af en grønnere og mere cirkulær økonomi.

Hovedformålet med dette kursus er at introducere de studerende til de metoder og værktøjer, der er tilgængelige, når en celle overføres fra et laboratorie- eller forskningsmiljø til pilot- og fuldskala. Den studerende, der følger dette kursus, bør derfor tilegne sig en bedre forståelse af interaktionerne mellem cellerne og hardwaren (reaktoren) og vil lære, hvordan man sigter mod optimale betingelser for cellernes vækst i stor skala. Emner som in situ produktfjernelse (ISPR), faststof- og gasfermentering, opskalering/nedskalering af en fermenteringsproces vil også blive fremhævet som en del af kurset. Der vil også blive lagt særlig vægt på at tilegne sig essentielle digitaliserings- og datahåndteringsfærdigheder.

Forelæsningerne vil blive afholdt af erfarne DTU-undervisere kombineret med gæsteundervisere fra industrien. Kurset inkluderer et besøg på et storskala fermenteringsanlæg.</Course_Objectives>

          <Contents>Elements included in this course are:
• reactor designs (the hardware) and reactor operating modes
• mass balances related to the different operating modes
• suspended growth systems vs. immobilized growth systems (biofilms)
• miniaturized reactor systems (high throughput fermentation)
• scale-up and scale-down of a process
• on-line sensors (both traditional and novel systems) and biosensors
• basic bioreactor control concepts
• on-line process control
• introduction to computational fluid dynamics and compartment models
• data (types of data, on-line versus off-line), proper handling of data
• different ways to use the available data (estimate parameters in a mechanistic model, extract process knowledge)
• basic concepts of artificial intelligence applied to fermentation data
• solvent recovery
• in situ product removal
• biofuels and biorefineries
• processes for converting waste into resources
• industrial symbiosis principles</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>The lectures will be given by experienced DTU teachers, combined with guest teachers from industry. The lectures are a mixture of theory and case study examples. Teaching is combined with exercises / problem solving sessions in class.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>In a linear economy, resources are converted into products, and the products will eventually end up as waste. In a circular economy, products and materials are recirculated and waste is eliminated as much as possible. A circular economy combined with renewable energy should yield a more sustainable system. In a sustainability context, fermentation processes have the ability to convert renewable resources to useful products. Therefore, fermentation can be considered as one of the cornerstones of a greener and more circular economy.

The main purpose of this course is to introduce students to the methods and tools that are available when transferring a cell from a lab or research environment to pilot and full scale. The student following this course should therefore acquire a better understanding of the interactions between the cells and the hardware (the reactor), and will learn how to aim for optimal conditions for the cells to grow in large scale. Topics such as  in situ product removal (ISPR), solid state and gas fermentation, scale up/scale down of a fermentation process will also be highlighted as part of the course. Special attention will also be paid to acquiring essential digitalization and data handling skills.

The lectures will be given by experienced DTU teachers, combined with guest teachers from industry. The course includes a visit to a large-scale fermentation facility.</Course_Objectives>
