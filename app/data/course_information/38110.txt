        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>The focus of the course is to analyse how to involve different actors and artefacts in the design process, where the goal is to create knowledge-building dialogues. We will analyze the historical development of actor-involvement and see how this has progressed from looking at a user as an object of study or test person to looking at use practice and other actors as 'co-creators'. We will examine and discuss a range of approaches to user involvement, such as: usability, design labs, generative sessions, video, performance, design games, and participatory innovation.

More concretely, this course explores how to design, facilitate, and evaluate co-creative processes in complex, interdisciplinary contexts. It integrates participatory design, universal design, creativity theory, and emerging technologies (e.g., AI-assisted co-creation) to prepare students to lead innovation processes in real-world settings, focused on generating inclusive solutions.

Students will engage in hands-on workshops, real stakeholder (incl. case owners and end-users) collaboration, and iterative design experimentation, culminating in a validated concept solution.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Reading and analysing texts, lectures and cases, and facilitating exercises</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Literature>Course literature will be uploaded to Learn throughout the course on a week-by-week basis</Course_Literature>
          <Course_Objectives>The course aims to provide students with a solid understanding of dialogue-based methods for involving stakeholders and using artefacts in innovation processes. It also develops students’ practical skills in facilitating co-creation workshops.

Throughout the course, students will learn to analyse, select, develop, and test techniques and tools that support the active involvement of practitioners in both real and simulated design processes. Key approaches include user-driven innovation, participatory design, universal design, and co-creation.

Students are expected to demonstrate a deep understanding of these approaches and critically reflect on their significance in relation to staged dialogue, the broader design process, and resulting outcomes.

The course provides a strong foundation for designing and facilitating interdisciplinary collaboration involving diverse stakeholders. This includes activities related to front-end innovation, idea generation, synthesis, and the testing and evaluation of design solutions and performances. Students will also build a theoretical basis for reflection, grounded in the course literature.

Finally, the course incorporates real-world challenges, where students work in teams to plan and carry out co-creation activities addressing a variety of complex problems.</Course_Objectives>
