        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>Kursets udgangspunkt er at etnografisk analyse kan ses som eksperimentel praksis. Ved at give deltagerne mulighed for selv at benytte analytiske protokoller, hvis effekter uddybes af erfarne forskere, er formålet med kurset at åbne nye veje hen imod bedre etnografiske analyser. Kurset er baseret på antologien ”Experimenting with Ethnography: A companion to Analysis” (Ballestero, Winthereik, red. 2021), hvori forfatterne bruger egen praksis til at udvikle veje til eksperimentel etnografisk analyse. Fremfor at anskue analyse som en abstrakt og intellektuel relation til data, er formålet med kurset at formidle en engageret og kreativ praksis for forskere. Et vigtigt udgangspunkt for bogen er at problematisere, at en fast grænse mellem ”feltet” (dataindsamling) og ”skrivebordet” (analyse, teoretisering) skulle eksistere eller automatisk være meningsfuld. Kurset arbejder ganske som bogen med analytiske protokoller og forbinder sig dermed til historiske eksperimenter som værende af interesse for samfundsvidenskab og humaniora (Rheinberger 1997; Latour 1999; Tilley 2011; Kowal, Radin &amp; Reardon 2013; Davies et al. 2018; Wolfe 2018 i Ballestero &amp; Winthereik 2021). Kurset vil dække en bred vifte af emner og metoder såsom multimodale tilgange, multispecies etnografi, sensorisk modalitet, afkolonisering af metodologi og samarbejdsformer etc. Teoretisk er kurset funderet i STS og i mindre grad antropologi. Centrale spørgsmål, som deltagerne inviteres til at engagere sig i: Hvordan begrænses vi af traditionelle måder at tænke analyse på, når vi sidder med egne data? Hvordan påvirker eksperimentelle tilgange til analyse vidensproduktion, databehandling og de empiriske felter, vi finder relevante? Hvordan kan vi som forskere skabe kreative rum i det daglige, der åbner for samarbejde? Hvilke ny indsigter i data kan vi opnå ved at anvende protokoller som fremgangsmåde?</Contents>

          <Contents>The course introduces the concept of analysis as an experimental practice. By offering the participants the opportunity to themselves use a set of analytic protocols, the effects of which will be discussed and elaborated by peers and experienced scholars, the aim of the course is to open new paths of doing ethnographic analysis. The structure of the course is based on chapters from the anthology Experimenting with Ethnography (Ballestero, Winthereik, eds. 2021) where the writers all use their own practice to suggest ways of experimenting with ethnographic analysis. Rather than approaching analysis as an abstract and solely intellectual practice, the aim of the book and its protocols is to convey a concrete mode of action and creative practice for researchers. An important point of departure for the book is problematizing that a fixed boundary between “the field” (data collection) and “the desk” (analysis, theorizing) exists or is automatically meaningful. The concept of the analytical protocols links to the history of experimental settings as a site of interest in the social sciences and humanities (Rheinberger 1997; Latour 1999; Tilley 2011; Kowal, Radin &amp; Reardon 2013; Davies et al. 2018; Wolfe 2018 in Ballestero &amp; Winthereik 2021). The course will cover a broad range of topics such as multimodal approaches, multispecies ethnography, sensory modality, decolonization of methodology and modes of collaboration. Theoretically, the course is based on STS and to a lesser extent anthropology. Participants are invited to engage in and reflect on questions such as:
How are we confined by traditional ways of thinking about analysis in approaching our own data? 
How do experimental approaches to analysis inform the production of knowledge, the treatment of data, as well as the empirical sites we enter?  
How can we as researchers nurture a space of play opening up ways of experimental thinking?  
What new insights about our data and our object of study can we achieve by applying concrete protocols with method for experimentation.</Contents>
