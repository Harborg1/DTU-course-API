        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>The course will equip PhD students with advanced knowledge across a range of aquatic research disciplines, including oceanography, food web interactions, ecophysiology, fisheries management, aquaculture and fisheries technology, and ecological modelling. The students will gain understanding of these concepts through discussions of selected articles, presentations of their own PhD research, and presentations by other early career scientists and PI’s. This will provide students with interdisciplinary competences and the ability to put their own research work into a broader context. Students will be responsible for chairing one session each semester.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>The course consists of approx. 20 seminar sessions each lasting one hour. 
Students are expected to actively participate in these sessions. They have the responsibility to present once every semester and to chair a session once every two semesters.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>The course is set up as a seminar series.
Students will participate in seminar sessions, which involve presentations of ongoing research, discussing selected articles, and engaging with peers and guests
Each student participating in the course will present once every semester. Students can present their own PhD research, as well as a scientific topic related to their research. 
Each student participating in the course will design and chair a seminar session once every two semesters 
Through presentations and discussions, students will learn to put their research into a broader context, understanding how different fields within marine science interconnect.</Course_Objectives>
