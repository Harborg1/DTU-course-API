        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>The course begins with an introduction to fundamental polymer properties and methods, as well as introduction workshop on a relevant topic. Afterwards, each group visits a company, where they are presented with a real-world polymer-related challenge. Based on this, students plan and organize their own experimental work to address the problem. Prerequisites for the individual problems may vary to suit the profiles of individual students.
Most of the course is spent performing hands-on laboratory work at DTU (Danish Polymer Centre) and/or at the company site.
Active learning is a central element of the course. Students receive regular feedback, participate in peer reviews of each other’s presentations, and benefit from open office hours to discuss their progress. The course concludes with group presentation submissions and in plenum presentation session with feedback on the final day. These activities provide a broad experience and a comprehensive understanding of key areas in polymer science and industrial applications.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Project work in groups. As a start of the course, each group visits a company that presents a polymer-related problem/challenge. Presentation of the project work in plenum concludes the course work.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>The overall goal of the course is to introduce students to the polymer-related field through a hands-on, industry-oriented project work. The course begins with an introduction to polymer characterization methods, introductory workshop, and a potential company visit, where an industrial challenge is being presented, and continues with project-based group work on a relevant polymer challenge. Students engage in practical investigations using experimental methods commonly applied in industry, gaining insight into the polymer product development.</Course_Objectives>
          <Remark>We kindly ask that any cancellations be made well in advance, as late changes can affect the distribution of group work for the participating companies.</Remark>
