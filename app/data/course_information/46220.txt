        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>I kurset vil vi diskutere og analysere de mest centrale problemstillinger knyttet til den grønne energiomstilling i VUØer. Disse økonomier adskiller sig fra højindkomstlande ved lavere økonomisk og institutionel udvikling, knaphed på finansielle og entreprenørielle ressourcer, større uligheder, større institutionel diversitet, en højere grad af statslig kontrol samt højere risici. Dekarbonisering af energisektoren i VUØer er en omfattende og multidimensionel udfordring – teknisk, finansielt, økonomisk, politisk og socialt.

De udfordringer, som behandles i kurset, omfatter blandt andet følgende: Der kræves omfattende økonomiske transformationer i industrier, forretningsmodeller, kompetencer og markeder for at udvikle en bæredygtig energisektor. At operere i disse økonomiers forretningsmiljøer indebærer særlige udfordringer for multinationale virksomheder, og omvendt kan tilstedeværelsen og engagementet fra multinationale virksomheder skabe særlige udfordringer i forhold til landenes udviklingsmål. De måder, hvorpå lokale virksomheder og institutioner inddrages, har langsigtede konsekvenser for landenes opgraderings- og udviklingsbaner. Transformation af styrings- og reguleringsrammer fremhæves ofte som afgørende for den grønne energiomstilling, herunder etableringen af understøttende institutionelle rammer, administrativ effektivitet og effektive incitamentsstrukturer. Der er desuden behov for videre udvikling af en række teknologier til produktion, transmission og lagring af energi for at muliggøre en omkostningseffektiv dekarbonisering af energisektoren. Finansieringsbehovet for den grønne energiomstilling i VUØer er blevet anslået til at være syv gange højere end det nuværende investeringsniveau i 2030, og de store finansieringsgab kræver innovative finansieringsløsninger og større privat deltagelse. Sociale udfordringer omfatter blandt andet at opretholde en bred samfundsmæssig opbakning og håndtere den ujævne fordeling af omstillingens omkostninger og gevinster.</Contents>

          <Course_Objectives>Kurset giver de studerende et overblik over og forståelse for de særlige økonomiske, politiske, teknologiske og sociale udfordringer, der er forbundet med den grønne energiomstilling i vækst- og udviklingsøkonomier (VUØer). Dekarbonisering af energisektoren, som globalt står for omkring tre fjerdedele af udledningerne, er afgørende for at begrænse klimaforandringer. I dag står VUØer for hovedparten af de globale CO₂-udledninger, og udledningerne forventes at stige. Samtidig er disse lande blandt de mest sårbare over for klimaforandringer. På grund af udfordringernes omfang er VOØer også blevet store – men ofte risikofyldte – investeringsdestinationer for virksomheder fra højindkomstlande inden for grønne industrier. I dette kursus vil vi diskutere udfordringerne ved den grønne energiomstilling i VUØer ud fra forskellige interessentperspektiver, herunder nationale regeringer, lokale samfund, internationale organisationer og multinationale virksomheder.</Course_Objectives>

          <Contents>In the course, we will discuss and analyze the most pertinent issues related to green energy transition in emerging economies. Emerging economies are distinct from high-income countries with their lower economic and institutional development, financial and entrepreneurial scarcities, greater inequalities, higher institutional diversity, higher extent of state control, higher risks, etc. Decarbonizing the energy sector in emerging economies is a massive and multi-dimensional challenge in technical, financial, economic, policy, and social terms. 

The challenges we will discuss in the course include the following: wide-ranging economic transformations in industries, business models, capabilities, and markets are required to develop a sustainable energy sector. Operating in the business environments in these economies represents challenges for multinational firms, and conversely, the presence and involvement of multinational firms represent special challenges for the countries’ development goals. The modes of involvement of local firms and institutions have long-term implications for the countries’ upgrading trajectories. Transformation of governance and regulatory frameworks are often highlighted as primary determinants of green energy transition, including supportive institutional frameworks, administrative efficiency, and effective incentive systems. Further development of a range of technologies for generation, transmission and storage of energy is required for cost-efficient decarbonization of the energy sector. Financing needs for green energy transition in emerging economies have been estimated to be seven times the current level of investment by 2030, and huge financing gaps call for innovative and private schemes. Social challenges include maintaining a broad societal coalition and managing the uneven distributions of transition costs and benefits.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>This course employs a mix of lectures and exercises. Lectures will cover core concepts and theories, and students participate in interactive discussions. Integrative cases, data, and reports will be discussed during the lectures to illustrate the applicability of the concepts and theories introduced in lectures. As part of the lectures, students will work in smaller groups to focus on the applicability of the theories and concepts based on data and cases.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Literature>The students will be provided with required readings and lecture notes through DTU Learn. We will also use additional literature (downloadable from the library), short videos, relevant databases, reports/analyses, and cases.</Course_Literature>
          <Course_Objectives>The course provides students with an overview and understanding of the special economic, policy, technological, and social challenges associated with green energy transition in emerging economies. Decarbonizing the energy sector, which globally accounts for three-quarters of emissions, is fundamental in climate change mitigation. Today, emerging economies account for the main share of global carbon emissions, and emissions are projected to increase. They are also among the most vulnerable to climate change. Due to the scale of these challenges, emerging economies have also become large while often risky commercial investment destinations for high-income country firms in green industries. In this course, we will discuss the challenges of green energy transition in emerging economies from different stakeholder perspectives, including national governments, local societies, international organizations, and multinational firms.</Course_Objectives>
