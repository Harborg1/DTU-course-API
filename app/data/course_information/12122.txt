        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>Afløbssystemer og urban regnvandshåndtering; skybrud; regnoversvømmelse; kloakoverløb; dimensionering af nye systemkomponenter; hydraulik; byernes hydrologi; tilbagestuvning; modellering; forenkling; st. Venants ligninger; fællessystemer, kloakseparering, naturbaserede løsninger (LAR, SUDS, LIDS, WSUD...); nedbørsstatistikker; gentagelsesperiode; kilder til forurening; effekter af klimaændringer, tilpasning til klimaændringer, regnvandsopsamling og vandcirkularitet, realtidsmodellering og styring.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Forelæsninger, øvelser, computersimuleringer, ekskursioner, projektarbejde i grupper, mundtlige gruppeoplæg, rapportskrivning og quizzer.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>Målet er at give deltagerne generelle færdigheder og viden, der er nødvendig for at designe, analysere og tilpasse afløbs- og regnvandssystemer i byer under påvirkning af voksende urbanisering og kraftigere nedbørshændelser samt stigende krav til forureningskontrol og behov for klimatilpasning af byområder. Der gives viden om de vigtigste hydrauliske og hydrologiske processer samt statistikker om ekstrem nedbør, regnbetingede forureningsudledninger og regnoversvømmelser. Der gives færdigheder i de grundlæggende principper for dimensionering af afløbs- og regnvandssystemer samt i brugen af state-of-the-art software til statisk og dynamisk simulering af disse systemer under kraftig regn (f.eks. SCALGO, MIKE+). Kurset fokuserer på både vandkvantitet og -kvalitet og vil introducere både traditionelle og nye løsninger til håndtering af regnvand – herunder tekniske anlæg, naturbaserede løsning (NBS) og Sponge Cities, samt nye digitale dataprodukter og herunder principper for monitering, realtids styring og digitale tvillinger.</Course_Objectives>
          <DTU_Remark>Kurset henvender sig til miljø- og bygningsingeniørstuderende samt andre studerende, der er interesserede i infrastruktur i byer, computersimuleringer, anvendt hydrologi og hydraulik, byplanlægning og klimatilpasning</DTU_Remark>
        </Txt>
        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>Urban drainage and stormwater management; cloudburst; pluvial flooding; sewer overflow; dimensioning new system components; hydraulics; urban hydrology; backwater; modelling; simplification; st. Venants Equations; sewer separation; nature based solutions (LAR, SUDS, LIDS, WSUD...); rainfall statistics; return period; sources of pollution; effects of climate change, adapting to climate change, rainwater harvesting and water circularity, real time modelling and control.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Lectures, tutorials, computer simulations, excursions, project work in groups, oral group presentations, report writing and quizzes.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>The aim is to provide participants with general skills and knowledge necessary to design, analyze and adapt urban drainage and stormwater systems under the influence of growing urbanization and heavier rain intensities as well as increasing demands for pollution control  and needs for climate change adaptation of urban areas. Knowledge is provided on the most important hydraulic and hydrological processes, as well as statistics on extreme rainfall, wet-weather pollution discharges and pluvial flooding. Skills are provided in the fundamental principles for dimensioning urban drainage and stormwater systems as well as in the use of a state-of-the-art software for static and dynamic simulation of these systems under heavy rain (e.g. SCALGO, MIKE+). The course focuses on both water quantity and quality and will introduce both traditional and novel solutions to managing stormwater - including technical stormwater control measures, Nature Based Solutions (NBS) and Sponge Cities, as well as  new digital data products including principles for monitoring, and real-time control and digital twins.</Course_Objectives>
          <DTU_Remark>The course is aimed at environmental and civil engineering students, and other students interested in urban infrastructure, computer simulations, applied hydrology and hydraulics, urban planning and climate change adaptation</DTU_Remark>
