        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>Det levende system og dets tilsigtede formål definerer, hvordan bioreaktoren konstrueres. Derfor skal en klar beskrivelse af bioreaktoren, det levende system og deres tilsigtede anvendelse være fastlagt, før prototyping kan påbegyndes.

Prototyping indebærer design og opbygning af delsystemer, der måler nøgleparametre såsom opløst ilt, turbiditet, antal copepoder og algetæthed. Disse målinger danner grundlag for styring af bioreaktoren — eksempelvis regulering af næringstilførsel og temperatur i overensstemmelse med det levende systems behov. De enkelte delsystemer integreres efterfølgende i én samlet funktionel enhed.

Et multi-parametrisk sensorsystem er ønskeligt, da væksten af levende organismer afhænger af mange indbyrdes relaterede faktorer (f.eks. pH, temperatur og tilstedeværelse af prædatorer). De studerende har betydelig frihed til at vælge, hvilke sensorer der skal implementeres, og hvordan de indsamlede data skal bruges til at styre systemet. Udfordringen ligger i at afgøre, hvilke parametre der er mest kritiske for at opretholde det levende system og opnå bioreaktorens formål på den mest effektive måde. De studerende kan også udforske muligheden for fjernovervågning og -styring via webbaserede grænseflader.

De studerende arbejder i store tværfaglige teams på 9–10 medlemmer. De studerende er selv ansvarlige for at danne deres teams; underviserne tildeler ikke teammedlemmer. Et velfungerende team samler en bred vifte af ingeniørprofiler for at imødekomme projektets tværfaglige krav. Projektet er studenterstyret, hvilket giver de studerende praktisk erfaring med at lede et stort team bestående af medlemmer med forskellige tekniske baggrunde. Hvert team støttes af en senior underviser, der vejleder i projektledelsesmæssige aspekter.

De studerende opfordres til at anvende AI-værktøjer (f.eks. Claude, Gemini, Copilot) som læringshjælpemidler til fejlfinding, forståelse af begreber og idégenerering. Alt indleveret arbejde skal dog afspejle den studerendes egen forståelse og skal kunne verificeres under evalueringsmøder.

Fysisk fremmøde er obligatorisk til alle laboratoriesessioner og evalueringsmøder. De studerende skal jævnligt tjekke kursets skema-/meddelelsesside for opdateringer.</Contents>

Bioreaktorer er derfor vigtige både for forskningen og industrien. Det er målet at lave en prototype dvs. bygge og designe en enhed, der kan (1) undersøge flere parametre i en bioreaktor (f.eks. pH-værdien, ilt, turbiditet, saltindhold, cellevækst mm.), (2) benytte disse parametre til at regulere tilførslen af [medium]/vand/næringsstoffer til bioreaktoren, og (3) forme den endelige dyrkning.         <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>The living system and its intended purpose define how the bioreactor is constructed. Therefore, a clear description of the bioreactor, the living system, and their intended use must be established before prototyping can begin.

Prototyping involves designing and building subsystems that measure key parameters such as dissolved oxygen, turbidity, copepod count, and algae density. These measurements form the basis for controlling the bioreactor — for example, regulating nutrient feed and temperature in accordance with the needs of the living system. The individual subsystems are subsequently integrated into a single functional device.

A multi-parametric sensor system is desirable, as the growth of living organisms depends on many interrelated factors (e.g., pH, temperature, and predator presence). Students have considerable freedom in choosing which sensors to implement and how to use the collected data to control the system. The challenge lies in deciding which parameters are most critical for sustaining the living system and achieving the bioreactor's purpose in the most efficient way. Students may also explore the possibility of remote monitoring and control via web-based interfaces.

Students work in large interdisciplinary teams of 9–10 members. Students are responsible for forming their own teams; teachers do not assign team members. A successful team brings together a broad range of engineering profiles to meet the multidisciplinary demands of the project. The project is student-led, giving students hands-on experience in managing a large team composed of members with diverse technical backgrounds. Each team is supported by a senior teacher who provides guidance on project management aspects.

Students are encouraged to use AI tools (e.g., Claude, Gemini, Copilot) as learning aids for troubleshooting, understanding concepts, and generating ideas. However, all submitted work must reflect the student's own understanding and must be verifiable during review meetings.

Physical attendance is mandatory for all lab sessions and review meetings. Students must check the course Schedule/Announcements page frequently for updates.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>The course is a laboratory course that mainly requires students to design and build an autonomous system to control and study a given living system. It is expected that students work 8h/day in the laboratory.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Literature>Teaching hours, tutorials, and lesson notes. The Internet will be used as a source of information.</Course_Literature>
          <Course_Objectives>A bioreactor is typically used for production, water cleaning but also to model small ecosystems. Bioreactors are therefore central for research and industry. The objective is to “prototype”, that is design and build, a device that can (1) investigate some parameters in the bioreactor (e.g. pH, oxygen, turbidity, salinity, cell growth etc), (2) to use these parameters to regulate the feed of medium/water/nutrients to the bioreactor and (3) to model the resulting culture. The objective is also to get experience with using various eukaryotes such as algae, copepods and shrimps. The projects will be cross-disciplinary and require close collaboration between team members to succeed.  Therefore, an important objective is to apply project planning, and labour division strategies.</Course_Objectives>
