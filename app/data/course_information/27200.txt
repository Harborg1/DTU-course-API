        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>The course is structured in three connected phases covering systems biology, biological data generation, and computational analysis. It begins with systems-level biological foundations, including biological scales, representation of biological systems, regulatory interactions, network logic, sequence and structure principles, post-translational modifications, and experimental design with statistical inference. Students are introduced to how biological questions can be represented computationally as sequences, graphs, matrices, and model inputs.

The second part of the course focuses on major omics technologies and their analytical logic, including whole genome sequencing and 16S profiling, metagenomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, and metabolomics. Emphasis is placed on understanding what each technology measures, the structure of common biological data formats, technological limitations, and how measurement shapes downstream interpretation.

The final part of the course introduces computational modeling and modern analytical tools, including basic machine learning concepts, hidden Markov models, support vector machines, neural networks, pathway and network analysis, knowledge bases, protein language models, and multiomics integration through latent representations and autoencoder-based concepts. Throughout the course, students work with biological datasets in guided exercises, apply computational tools, discuss limitations of models and technologies, and critically evaluate AI-supported analyses. A group project integrates multiple course themes and concludes with oral presentations.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>13-week course with weekly 4-hour sessions combining lectures, active learning, guided group exercises, and structured discussion. Teaching uses computational notebooks and biological datasets. Students work in groups, submit assignments, and complete a final project presentation.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>The course provides a structured foundation for understanding biological systems through data, models, and computation. It introduces students to systems-level biological reasoning across molecular, cellular, and organismal scales, and develops the ability to connect biological questions with computational representations, measurement technologies, and multi-omics analytical approaches. The course covers biological regulation, sequence and structure principles, experimental design, omics technologies, statistical reasoning, machine learning, and modern AI-based analytical tools. Emphasis is placed on recognizing common analytical principles across biological data types, understanding how technologies constrain inference, and critically evaluating models and computational outputs. The course is designed as a broad cognitive foundation, preparing students for advanced study in bioengineering, systems biology, computational biology, and data-driven biotechnology.</Course_Objectives>
