        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>I kurset gennemgår vi den nødvendige del af vektor- og tensoranalysen, der anvendes i undersøgelsen af biomekaniske problemstillinger. Den matematiske formalisme anvendes efterfølgende til at formulere de grundlæggende bevarelseslove og forskellige typer af konstitutive ligninger. Tilsammen danner disse principper basis for al biomekanisk analyse og modellering. Eksempler inkluderer elastiske, visko-elastiske og hyperelastiske vævsmaterialer. Desuden gennemgås anatomien og fysiologien af menneskets hofteled, store sener og karvæggen i arterier/vener.
Vi vil læse og diskutere videnskabelige artikler, der beskriver biomekaniske problemstillinger for at give dig indsigt i hvorledes forskning udføres inden for disse områder. Der vil desuden være gæsteforelæsninger fra eksterne forskere der arbejder med forskellige aspekter af biomekanik.
Endelig omfatter kurset eksperimentelt arbejde, hvor du skal bearbejde data til at analysere en given biomekanisk problemstilling. På Panum Instituttet vil der blive demonstreret metoder til opsamling af biomekanisk data. Gennem kurset vil du få tid til at opstille finite element modeller til at analysere biomekaniske problemstillinger ud fra eksperimentelle data. Du skal aflevere skriftlige rapporter for øvelserne, som beskriver dine resultater og konsekvensen af disse.</Contents>

          <Course_Objectives>Målet med kurset er at give en grundig introduktion til fænomenologien og fundamentale principper i inden for mekanik, med fokus på vævsbiomekanik. Vi vil særligt arbejde med menneskets hofteled, store sener samt karvæggen i arterier/vener. Der undervises både i anatomien og fysiologien såvel som modellering af den mekaniske opførsel i de nævnte strukturer.
Som studerende skal du skrive 3 grupperapporter (3-4 studerende per gruppe), som beskriver øvelser/modeller udført i kurset. Der vil også være en "journal club" hvor du får lejlighed til at præsentere en videnskabelig artikel (i grupper), denne indgår på ingen måde i den samlede bedømmelse.</Course_Objectives>

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>In this course we will go through the necessary mathematics of vector and tensor analysis used for solving biomechanical problems. This mathematical formalism is subsequently used to formulate the fundamental conservation laws and different types of constitutive equations. Together these principles form the basis of all biomechanical modeling. Examples include elastic, visco-elastic, and hyper elastic tissue materials. In addition, the anatomy and physiology of the human hip joint, large tendons and the blood vessel wall of arteries/veins will be covered.
We will read and discuss scientific papers describing various biomechanical problems to provide you with insight into how research is performed within these areas. Furthermore, there will be guest lectures by external researchers working with various aspects of biomechanics.
The course also incorporates experimental work, where you will work with data to analyze a given biomechanical problem. Demonstrations of biomechanical data collection will take place at the Panum Institute. During the course you will be given time to perform finite element modeling of biomechanical problems based on experimental data. You must hand in written reports describing your findings and the implications of the model results.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Lectures, reading papers (journal club), problem solving, finite element modelling, homework problems and reports</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Literature>The course material is primary parts taken from e-books available via DTU FindIt:
1) J. D. Humphrey, S. L. O'Rourke: An Introduction to Biomechanics - solids and fluids, analysis and design, 2nd edition. Springer, 2015
2) F. Irgens: Continuum Mechanics. Springer, 2008
3) S. C. Cowin, S. B. Doty: Tissue Mechanics. Springer, 2007
4) J. D. Humphrey: Cardiovascular Solid Mechanics - cells, tissues and organs. Springer, 2002</Course_Literature>
          <Course_Objectives>The objective of the course is to give a thorough introduction to the phenomenology and fundamental principles of mechanics, with focus on tissue biomechanics. We will particularly be investigating the human hip joint, large tendons, and blood vessel walls of arteries/veins. Both the anatomy and physiology as well as modeling of the mechanical behavior of these structures will be taught.
As a student, you will write 3 group reports (3-4 students per group) describing exercises/models performed as part of the course. The reports must be written to indicate which group member mainly contributed to a given section/element in the report (individualization). Reports are handed in throughout the course. Further, you have to submit 3 smaller homework assignments.
The reports and homework assignments are mandatory and must be handed in to qualify for the exam (exam prerequisite). They also constitute "ongoing tests" and contribute together with the final exam to the overall evaluation, where the primary weight is on the exam.
There will also be a "journal club" where you will have the opportunity to present a scientific paper (in groups). The journal club does not contribute to the evaluation in any way.</Course_Objectives>
