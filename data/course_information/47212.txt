        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>Forskellige beregningsmetoder til simulering af materialer vil blive opdelt i tre hovedemner, der hvert dækkes i løbet af fire uger. Følgende metoder vil blive introduceret i kurset: 1) termodynamiske beregninger og diffusionssimuleringer, 2) Monte Carlo-simuleringer, og 3) egenskabsmodellering ved homogenisering ved brug af finite element-metoden. Inden for hver 4-ugers periode vil de studerende først lære om de forskellige simuleringsværktøjer via forelæsninger og små øvelser. Derefter vil der blive introduceret et gruppeprojekt for hvert emne. Gruppeprojekterne vil anvende simuleringsmetoderne på et materialevidenskabeligt problem. De studerende vil bruge de lærte simuleringsværktøjer til at løse specifikke problemer med komplekse faseligevægte, diffusionsinduceret fasetransformation, mikrostrukturel evolution under kornvækst og mekaniske egenskaber. Den simulerede mikrostruktur vil blive sammenlignet med rigtige mikrostrukturer. De studerende vil yderligere forudsige materialers egenskaber baseret på de simulerede mikrostrukturer. I slutningen af hver 4-ugers periode vil de studerende præsentere projektets resultater i form af en rapport eller en gruppepræsentation for underviserne og de øvrige studerende.</Contents>

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>Different computational materials simulation methods will be divided into three main topics each being discussed for four weeks. The following methods will be introduced in the course: (1) thermodynamic calculations and diffusion simulations, (2) Monte Carlo simulations, and (3) property modelling by homogenization using the finite element method. Within each 4-week period, the students will first learn about the different simulation tools via lectures and small exercises. Then a group project will be introduced for each topic. The students will apply the simulation methods to a materials science problem. The students will utilize the simulation tools to address specific problems of complex phase equilibria, diffusion induced phase transformation, microstructural evolution during grain growth and mechanical properties. The simulated microstructure will be compared with real microstructures. The students will further predict materials properties based on the simulated microstructures. At the end of each 4-week period, the students will present the results of the project in the form of a report or a group presentation to the teachers and the other students.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>The course will contain three main topics discussing different materials simulation methods. The course will be based on lectures, simulation exercises and project work in groups.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Literature>Course materials will be made available online during the course.</Course_Literature>
          <Course_Objectives>In this course, the student will obtain an understanding of basic principles behind numerical simulations of microstructure evolution in crystalline materials (oxides and metals) induced by chemical, mechanical or thermal external loads, including the associated changes in material properties, such as microstructure, electrical and thermal conductivity, elasticity etc. This course gives an introduction to various materials simulation methods from micro to macro scale and is thus priming the students who wish to go deeper into computer simulation and/or design of structural or functional materials in later courses.</Course_Objectives>
          <DTU_Remark>Are you interested in learning computer simulations to understand materials systems and to drive future technology and innovation in materials science and engineering? If so, this is the course for you.</DTU_Remark>
