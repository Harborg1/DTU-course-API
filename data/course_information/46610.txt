        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>I dag foregår udnyttelse af vindenergi hovedsagelig i stor skala ved hjælp af MW vindmøller, men den tekniske fysik og de aerodynamiske principper er stort set de samme som for meget små vindmøller. Ideen er at opbygge en vindmølledrevet bil til at køre så hurtigt som muligt i direkte modvind, bygge den mest energi producerende vindmølle eller den bedste flyder til flydende vindmøller. De studerende introduceres i første omgang til konkurrencerne, teorien for og virkemåden af de eksisterende vindbil/turbine/flyder. Opgaver rettet mod at fremhæve vigtige grundlæggende fysiske principper og funktion af både vindbil/turbine/flyder bliver løst af studerende. Sideløbende med forelæsningerne vil de studerende gennemgå tidligere byggede biler/vindmøller/flyder og baseret på nye ideer/begreber, bygges der en ny bil/vindmølle/flyder eller dele heraf. Centrale dele af hhv. bil/turbine/flyder struktur bliver udvalgt på baggrund af en analyse af aero-mekanikken og komponenterne opdateres i grupper for hvert udvalgt område (aerodynamik, vinger, svøb, nav konstruktion, transmission, kontrolsystem, instrumentering, elektrisk system, kompositmaterialer, flyder koncept, osv.). Fagområder der er relateret til design og konstruktion tages i anvendelse og er for eksempel, aerodynamik, maskin- elementer, fiber kompositter, eksperimentel afprøvning, flyder stabilitet, osv. Designopgaverne bliver tilpasset den forudgående viden og ambitionsniveauet af de studerende. Et centralt mål er integrationen af de forskellige komponenter i en funktionel og effektiv vindbil/turbine/flyder.  Før konkurrencerne bliver de udviklede/forbedrede konstruktioner testet i felten.</Contents>

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>Today wind energy conversion takes place mainly on large scale using MW wind turbines, but the engineering physics and the aerodynamic principles are largely the same as for very small wind turbines. The idea is to build a wind powered car to drive as fast as possible in direct headwind, to build the most producing wind turbine or the most stable floater for floating wind turbines. The students are initially introduced to the competitions, the theory for and mechanics of the existing car/turbine/floater. Assignments targeted at highlighting key fundamental physics and behavior of the wind car/turbine/floater are solved by the students. In parallel with course lectures, previously built wind cars/turbines/floaters are reviewed and based on new ideas / concepts, design and construction of a new or part of a new wind car/turbine/floater is done. Identified core issues for the car/turbine/floating structure are selected based on analysis of the aeromechanics and are solved in groups for each selected area (aerodynamics, wings, shroud, hub mechanics, transmission, control, instrumentation, electrical system, composite material, floater concepts, etc.). The disciplines related to design and construction are for example, aerodynamics, machinery elements, fiber composite, experimental testing, stability of floaters,  etc. The design tasks are adapted to the prior knowledge and ambition of the students. A key objective is the integration of the various components into a functional and effective wind car/turbine/floater construction. Prior to the competitions the developed designs are tested in the field.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Project work, exercises and lectures</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>To introduce students to conversion of wind energy, specifically in relation to the fastest possible speed of a wind driven car, the best producing wind turbine or the most stable floater for floating wind turbines. The objective is to let the students apply learned theory into practice in solving a design task leading to construction / improvement of a wind car/wind turbine/floater. The final goal is to participate in international competitions.</Course_Objectives>
          <DTU_Remark>The course will take place in the spring and ends up with participation in international Wind Energy Competition. DTU participates in Racing Aeolus (Den Helder, The Netherlands), the International Small Wind Turbine Competition, ISWTC and the Floating Wind Challenge for building floating wind turbines.</DTU_Remark>
          <Remark>The course is specifically aiming at designing and building for participating in international competitions on the fastest wind-turbine powered car, the most producing wind turbine or the best floater. The first part of the course consists of a series of lectures and assignments aimed at familiarizing the students with the key physics and technical issues involved for both windcar/wind turbine/floater. Armed with the knowledge and tools obtained in the first part, the students proceed to the last part of the course, where the student / group of students (3-5) identify a core issue for the vehicle/turbine/floater structure/design and thereafter plan and organize further activities to design, build, test and integrate the components. The course is part of Blue Dot project https://blue-dot.dtu.dk/english/dtuwindracers</Remark>
