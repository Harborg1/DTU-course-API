        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>Den første uge af kurset består af en række forelæsninger, øvelser og laboratoriearbejde samt en éndags felttur på forskningsskibet "Havfisken". Forelæsningerne introducerer nøgleprincipper inden for fysisk, biologisk og kemisk oceanografi, fiskebiologi og populationsøkologi. Øvelserne og laboratoriearbejdet fokuserer på vandets fysik og økologi af marine plankton og fisk. Under feltturen med "Havfisken" vil de studerende bruge in situ sensorer og fiskeredskaber til at måle fysiske og kemiske egenskaber i havvand og bestemme diversiteten af plankton- og fiskesamfundene. I de følgende to uger vil de studerende arbejde på et gruppeprojekt, enten eksperimentelt i laboratoriet eller en teoretisk undersøgelse. Baseret på dette projektarbejde i grupper vil de lave en præsentation og en poster.

Projektarbejdet vil opmuntre de studerende til at anvende de færdigheder og den viden, de har opnået i 1. uge, og udføre en dybdegående undersøgelse, der vil blive faciliteret af kursusunderviserne. Studerende kan vælge projekter fra forskellige overordnede emner, men også inkludere yderligere aspekter, hvis de ønsker det: Emnerne inkluderer

1) kemiske og fysiske egenskaber i vandkolonnen,
2) effekter af temperatur på fisks energiforbrug,
3) biologiske træk af fiskearter med forskellige livshistoriestrategier, og
4) hvordan zooplankton og fytoplankton tilpasser deres adfærd for at håndtere miljømæssige udfordringer.</Contents>

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>The first week of the course consists of a series of lectures, exercises, and lab work, plus a one-day field excursion on the research vessel “Havfisken”.  The lectures introduce key principles in physical, biological, and chemical oceanography, fish biology, and population ecology. The exercises and lab work focus on water physics, ecology of marine plankton and fish. During the field excursion with ‘Havfisken’, students will use in situ sensors and fishing gear to measure physical and chemical properties of seawater and determine the diversity of the plankton and fish communities. In the following two weeks, the students will work on a group project, either experimental in the lab or a theoretical study. Based on this project work in groups, they will make a presentation and a poster.   

The project work will encourage students to apply the skills and knowledge gained in week 1 and carry out an in-depth investigation that will be facilitated by the course teachers. Students may choose projects from different overarching topics but also include additional aspects if they wish: Topics include

1) chemical and physical water column properties,
2) effects of temperature on fish energy use,
3) biological traits of fish species with different life history strategies, and
4) how zooplankton and phytoplankton adapt their behavior to cope with environmental challenges.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Lectures, laboratory exercises and a cruise in Øresund. The students work in groups and the course concludes with a PPT presentation and a poster, which will be presented and evaluated at a poster session.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>The course will provide students with a basic knowledge to evaluate general physical/chemical and biological principles in the ocean along with explaining their interactions. Thereby, the students will be able to reflect on the components of the SDG14 ‘life below water’ and their meaning. Specifically, students will gain knowledge on how to measure, transform, and analyse environmental data, investigate processes and drivers of population changes over time (such as growth, mortality, fecundity) for a diverse set of species (e.g., phytoplankton, zooplankton or fish). The students will gain knowledge that allows them to evaluate the structure of the water column and relate it to the functioning of aquatic ecosystems, and how living marine resources such as fish can be managed.</Course_Objectives>
