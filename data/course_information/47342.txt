        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>Kurset består af tre moduler: 1) grundlæggende om smeltede salte, 2) energikonverterings- og lagringsteknologier med smeltede salte og 3) nukleare energiteknologier baseret på smeltede salte som flydende brændsler, kølemidler og genbehandlingsmedier.

Kurset indledes med en introduktion til kemi, elektrokemi og materialevidenskab for smeltede salte. Relevante teknologier baseret på smeltede salte vil blive præsenteret med fokus på design, drift, principper, udfordringer og bæredygtige perspektiver, herunder nukleare reaktorer, brændselsceller, batterier, termisk lagring og nye power-to-x-processer.

Kursets indhold er tværfagligt, ingeniørorienteret og tilpasset studerende med forskellige faglige baggrunde.</Contents>

          <Course_Objectives>Saltsmelter er en klasse af ioniske forbindelser i flydende form, ofte ved forhøjede temperaturer. De udviser unikke egenskaber som ekstraordinær opløselighed af mange organiske og uorganiske forbindelser, overlegen termisk og radiolytisk stabilitet, høj elektrisk og varmeledningsevne og ekstremt lavt damptryk.

Kurset introducerer de grundlæggende forhold ved smeltede salte, herunder struktur, kemi, elektrokemi, fysiske egenskaber og materialemæssige udfordringer. Det giver en tværfaglig platform for forståelse af bæredygtige energiteknologier baseret på smeltede salte, som spænder over energiproduktion (f.eks. kernereaktorer) samt energilagring og -konvertering (f.eks. termisk energilagring, brændselsceller, batterier, CO₂-fangst og Power-to-X).

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>The course consists of three modules: (1) fundamentals of molten salts, (2) energy conversion and storage technologies involving molten salts, and (3) nuclear energy technologies based on molten salts as liquid fuels, coolants, and reprocessing media. 

The course will start by introduction of chemistry, electrochemistry, and materials science of molten salts. Relevant molten salt technologies will be presented with respect to design, operation, principles, challenges, and sustainable perspectives, including nuclear reactors, fuel cells, batteries, thermal storage, and novel power-to-x processes. 

The course content is crossdisciplinary, engineering orientated, and fitting wide backgrounds of students.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Lectures, class discussions, problem solving.
Guest lecturers from industry will be arranged from year to year.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Literature>Textbook: Molten Salts Chemistry from Lab to Applications, F. Lantelme and H. Groult (eds), Elsevier (2013). Available for free download via DTU Findit. Additional materials will be distributed at class. Additional materials will be distributed in class.</Course_Literature>
          <Course_Objectives>Molten salts are a versatile class of ionic compounds in liquid form often at elevated temperatures. They exhibit unique properties such as extraordinary solubility of many organic and inorganic compounds, superior thermal and radiolytic stability, high electrical and heat conductivity, and extremely low vapor pressures. Molten salts are hence an excellent engineering fluid for generation, conversion, and storage of sustainable energies. 

The course introduces the fundamentals of molten salts, including structure, chemistry, electrochemistry, physical properties, and materials challenges. It provides a multidisciplinary platform for understanding molten salt–based sustainable energy technologies, spanning energy generation (e.g., nuclear reactors) and energy storage and conversion (e.g., thermal energy storage, fuel cells, batteries, carbon capture, and Power-to-X).

By the end of the course, students will have a comprehensive understanding of molten salts and related energy technologies and will be equipped to contribute to research and innovation in this dynamic field.</Course_Objectives>
          <DTU_Remark>The course gives an all-round introduction to molten salts and related technologies for energy production, storage and conversion, e.g., nuclear reactors, thermal storage, batteries and other PtX processes.</DTU_Remark>
