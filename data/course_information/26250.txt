        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>The course introduces the core principles underlying modern learning systems for the molecular sciences. We describe methods to formalize, analyze and scale up each component of the design-build-test-learn cycle, and how these pieces can be combined into automated lab-in-the-loop systems. 

The course begins with probabilistic models of molecules and molecular data. We introduce methods to relate a molecule's chemical structure or sequence to its activity, including those based on structural similarity and on neural networks. We introduce probabilistic models of assays and synthesis, using exponential family distributions.

Next we cover methods for learning and inference. We explain methods to fit models to data, and how to apply these methods using probabilistic programming languages. We introduce methods to evaluate models' quality, focusing especially on quantifying uncertainty that comes from noisy assays or limited experiments.

Finally we study methods to design and plan experiments. We specify different scientific goals as objective functions for learning or optimization. We introduce methods for iteratively selecting candidates to test, and introduce bandit algorithms for trading off between exploration and exploitation.

Throughout, we focus on obtaining both a deep conceptual understanding and hands on experience. We explain how diverse problems in the molecular sciences (e.g. lead optimization, protein design, high throughput screening) can be formalized, analyzed, and implemented using core machine learning principles. We explain how diverse modern machine learning tools (e.g. Bayesian optimization, generative modeling, agents) instantiate these core principles.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Lectures, problem sets, in class problem solving, final project.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>This course focuses on experimental design and analysis for the molecular sciences using modern machine learning. Molecular space is vast, and navigating it efficiently requires a blend of algorithmic and chemical thinking. This course introduces the architecture of modern lab-in-the-loop and automated laboratory systems, with a focus on probabilistic foundations. It provides a rigorous mathematical and computational view of the design-build-test-learn cycle underpinning molecular discovery in chemistry, chemical biology and bioengineering.</Course_Objectives>
