        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>i. Almindelig præsentation af belægninger som formulerede kemiske produkter, herunder belægningsfunktioner, belægningsværdikæde og struktur-egenskab-ydeevneforhold.
ii. Grundlæggende belægningskomponenter: bindemidler, pigmenter, opløsningsmidler, tilsætningsstoffer, hærdningsmidler og deres roller i filmdannelse, stabilitet, påføring og endelig ydeevne.
iii. Bindemiddelkemi og filmdannelsesmekanismer, herunder termoplastiske og termohærdende systemer, tværbinding, hærdning, glasovergang, kohæsion, adhæsion og kemisk resistens.
iv. Pigmenter og fyldstoffer i belægningsformuleringer, herunder opacitet, farve, partikelstørrelse, pigmentvolumenkoncentration, kritisk pigmentvolumenkoncentration, barriereeffekter, forstærkning og defektdannelse.
v. Formuleringsdesignprincipper, herunder ingrediensvalg, kompatibilitet, pigment-bindemiddelbalance, målspecifikationer, procesbegrænsninger og ydeevnedrevne formuleringsstrategier.
vi. Pigmentdispersion og kolloid stabilitet i belægninger, herunder befugtning, deagglomeration, stabilisering, partikel-bindemiddelinteraktioner, sedimentation, flokkulering og dispersionskvalitet.
vii. Reologi af belægningsformuleringer, herunder viskositet, forskydningsfortynding, thixotropi, flydeevne, udjævning, sagging, opbevaringsstabilitet og anvendelsesafhængig filmopbygning.
viii. Korrosionsbeskyttelse ved belægninger, herunder barrierebeskyttelse, vedhæftning, overfladeforbehandling, vand- og iontransport, underfilmskorrosion, katodisk delaminering og belægningsfejl ved metal-belægningsgrænsefladen.
ix. Funktionelle og beskyttende belægninger, herunder antikorrosions-, antifouling-, brandbeskyttende, dekorative og andre industrielle belægningssystemer designet til specifikke miljøer og driftsforhold.
x. Testning, karakterisering og inspektion af belægninger, herunder tørfilmtykkelse, vedhæftning, hårdhed, mekanisk holdbarhed, barriereegenskaber, accelereret eksponering, felttestning og standardbaseret kvalificering.
xi. Udvikling og bæredygtighed af industriel belægning, herunder fejlfinding i formuleringer, produktdesignlogik, holdbarhed som en bæredygtighedsparameter, lovgivningsmæssige begrænsninger, digitalisering og oversættelse fra laboratorieformulering til industrielle belægningsprodukter.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Kurset undervises gennem en kombination af forelæsninger, interaktive diskussioner og problemløsningsøvelser. Forelæsningerne introducerer de videnskabelige principper og mekanistiske begreber, mens problemløsningsøvelserne opfordrer de studerende til kritisk tænkning og anvendelse af disse begreber på praktisk formulering og præstationsspørgsmål.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>Formålet med dette kursus er at give de studerende en struktureret forståelse af belægninger som konstruerede kemiske produkter, hvor råmaterialer, formuleringsdesign, forarbejdning, anvendelse, mikrostruktur og testning tilsammen bestemmer den endelige ydeevne. Kurset forbinder kemiteknik, anvendt kemi, kolloidvidenskab, produktdesign og industriel praksis ved at vise, hvordan bindemidler, pigmenter, opløsningsmidler, additiver, dispersion, reologi, overfladebehandling, hærdning, korrosionsbeskyttelse, funktionel ydeevne og kvalitetskontrol interagerer på tværs af belægningsværdikæden. De studerende vil lære at fortolke belægninger ikke kun som anvendte film, men som designede materialesystemer, hvis holdbarhed, udseende, funktionalitet, bæredygtighed og fejlmekanismer styres af målbare struktur-egenskab-ydeevne-forhold.</Course_Objectives>
          <Remark>Kurset underviser i belægninger som formulerede materialesystemer, hvor ingeniørvidenskab, kemi, forarbejdning, anvendelse og testning er direkte knyttet til den endelige ydeevne. De studerende bevæger sig fra råmaterialer og formuleringsprincipper til dispersion, reologi, korrosionsbeskyttelse, overfladebehandling, defekter, funktionelle belægninger og industriel kvalificering. Gennem hele kurset bliver de studerende presset til at tænke kritisk: ikke kun hvilken ingrediens eller proces der anvendes, men også hvorfor den anvendes, hvordan den ændrer belægningens mikrostruktur, og hvordan denne mikrostruktur styrer anvendelsesadfærd, holdbarhed, fejl og produktets ydeevne.</Remark>

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>i. Common presentation of coatings as formulated chemical products, including coating functions, coating value chain, and structure–property–performance relationships.
ii. Fundamental coating components: binders, pigments, solvents, additives, curing agents, and their roles in film formation, stability, application, and final performance.
iii. Binder chemistry and film formation mechanisms, including thermoplastic and thermoset systems, crosslinking, curing, glass transition, cohesion, adhesion, and chemical resistance.
iv. Pigments and fillers in coating formulations, including opacity, colour, particle size, pigment volume concentration, critical pigment volume concentration, barrier effects, reinforcement, and defect formation.
v. Formulation design principles, including ingredient selection, compatibility, pigment–binder balance, target specifications, processing constraints, and performance-driven formulation strategies.
vi. Pigment dispersion and colloidal stability in coatings, including wetting, deagglomeration, stabilization, particle–binder interactions, sedimentation, flocculation, and dispersion quality.
vii. Rheology of coating formulations, including viscosity, shear thinning, thixotropy, flow, leveling, sagging, storage stability, and application-dependent film build.
viii. Corrosion protection by coatings, including barrier protection, adhesion, surface pretreatment, water and ion transport, underfilm corrosion, cathodic delamination, and coating failure at the metal–coating interface.
ix. Functional and protective coatings, including anticorrosive, antifouling, fire-protective, decorative, and other industrial coating systems designed for specific environments and service conditions.
x. Testing, characterization, and inspection of coatings, including dry film thickness, adhesion, hardness, mechanical durability, barrier performance, accelerated exposure, field testing, and standard-based qualification.
xi. Industrial coating development and sustainability, including formulation troubleshooting, product design logic, durability as a sustainability parameter, regulatory constraints, digitalization, and translation from laboratory formulation to industrial coating products.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>The course is taught through a combination of lectures, interactive in-class activities, and problem-solving exercises. The lectures introduce the scientific principles and mechanistic concepts, while the problem-solving exercises push students to critically think and apply these concepts to practical formulation and performance questions.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>The objective of this course is to provide students with a structured understanding of coatings as engineered chemical products, where raw materials, formulation design, processing, application, microstructure, and testing collectively determine final performance. The course connects chemical engineering, applied chemistry, colloid science, product design, and industrial practice by showing how binders, pigments, solvents, additives, dispersion, rheology, surface preparation, curing, corrosion protection, functional performance, and quality control interact across the coating value chain. Students will learn to interpret coatings not only as applied films, but as designed material systems whose durability, appearance, functionality, sustainability, and failure mechanisms are governed by measurable structure–property–performance relationships.</Course_Objectives>
          <Remark>The course teaches coatings as formulated material systems where engineering, chemistry, processing, application, and testing are directly linked to final performance. Students move from raw materials and formulation principles to dispersion, rheology, corrosion protection, surface preparation, defects, functional coatings, and industrial qualification. Throughout the course, students are pushed to think critically: not only what ingredient or process is used, but why it is used, how it changes the coating microstructure, and how that microstructure controls application behavior, durability, failure, and product performance.</Remark>
