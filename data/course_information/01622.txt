        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>Ikke-lineære oscillatorer, Lagrange- og Hamilton systemer, dynamik på, og af, netværk samt systemer i tid og rum. Ikke-lineære partielle differentialligninger. Differentialligninger med tidsforsinkelser. Differentialalgebraiske ligninger. Anvendt bifurkationsanalyse, faseplansanalyse og kontrolteori.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Forelæsninger og gruppeøvelser</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>Kurset rummer dels teori dels praktiske eksempler med analyse af ikke-lineære oscillatorer, netværk af komplekse systemer, kontrolteori, problemer i rum og tid med anvendelser i ingeniørfagene og naturvidenskaben. Hvor hovedvægten i det indledende kursus i dynamisk systemer (01617) lå på den matematiske analyse af lokal dynamik, vil fokus i dette kursus være analysen af praktiske problemer i fysik, biologi og tekniske fag. Deltagerne vil lære både matematisk og numerisk analyse af systemers dynamik med både simpel og kompleks opførsel.</Course_Objectives>
        </Txt>
        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>Theory of nonlinear oscillations, Hamiltonian and Lagrangian theory, dynamics on and of networks, and systems in time and space. Nonlinear partial differential equations. Differential equations with time delays. Differential-algebraic equations. Applications of bifurcation theory and control theory.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Lectures and group work</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>This course covers both theory and practical techniques to analyze nonlinear oscillators, networks of complex systems, control theory, problems in space and time with applications in science and engineering . Whereas the focus in the introductory level dynamical systems course (01617) lies on the mathematical theory of local dynamics, we focus in this course on mastering the mathematical analysis applied to practical problems occurring in mathematics, physics, engineering, and life sciences.  Students will learn to master the analysis, both mathematical and numerical, of dynamical systems exhibiting both simple and complex behavior.</Course_Objectives>
