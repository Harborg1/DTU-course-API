        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>Kurset bygger videre på de problembaserede ingeniør- og prototypekompetencer, der er udviklet i kurset Design-build in Life Science (22180), og fokuserer på anvendelsen heraf i PK/PD-kontrollerede infection-on-chip eksperimenter.  
Baggrundsviden om bakteriel infektionsbiologi, PK/PD-principper og mikrofluidiske systemer formidles gennem forelæsninger, som udgør det teoretiske fundament for projekterne. Mikrofluidisk chip-teknologi gør det muligt at efterligne fysiologiske doseringsregimer af lægemidler og infektionsbetingelser.
Prototypen baseres på en infection-on-chip mikrofluidisk chip, der muliggør kontrollerede flow- og transportforhold og dermed forskellige strategier for lægemiddellevering. Systemet integrerer kontrol af pumper til levering og blanding af antibiotika kombineret med sensorbaserede målinger med kameraer og optiske sensorer. Dette muliggør realtids måling af bakterievækst og eliminering gennem hele eksperimentet. En mikrocontroller anvendes til dataopsamling ved hjælp af basal scripting.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Kurset er projektbaseret og anvender en kombination af forelæsninger, øvelser og laboratoriearbejde, hvor de studerende arbejder i grupper.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>Kurset giver de studerende grundlæggende viden om bakterielle infektioner og infektionssygdomme samt basale principper inden for farmakokinetik (PK) og farmakodynamik (PD) relateret til antimikrobiel behandling. Parallelt hermed vil de studerende designe og bygge et mikrofluidisk system, der understøtter dannelse af bakterielle biofilm. Systemet anvendes til at studere doseringsregimer af antimikrobielle behandlinger som efterligner humane PK/PD-profiler. Prototypen vil muliggøre dataopsamling i realtid samt modellering af behandlingsforløb.</Course_Objectives>

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>This course builds on the problem-based engineering and prototyping skills developed in the course Design-build in Life Science (22180) and focuses on applying them to PK/PD-controlled infection-on-chip experiments. 
Background knowledge on bacterial infection biology, PK/PD principles and microfluidic systems will be provided through lectures that deliver the theoretical foundation for the projects. Microfluidic chip technology allows mimicking physiological drug exposure and infection conditions.
The prototype will be built around an infection-on-chip microfluidic chip, which allows controlled flow and transport conditions, enabling different drug delivery strategies. It will integrate pump control for drug mixing and treatment delivery, combined with sensor-based monitoring such as cameras and optical sensors to enable real-time monitoring of bacterial growth and eradication throughout the experiment. A microcontroller will be employed to acquire real-time data using basic scripting.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>The course is project based and uses a mixture of lectures, exercises and lab work where the students work in groups.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>This course provides students with foundational knowledge of bacterial infections and infectious diseases, as well as basic principles of pharmacokinetics (PK) and pharmacodynamics (PD) for antimicrobial therapy. In parallel, students will design and build a microfluidic device that supports bacterial biofilm production. The device will be used to study antimicrobial treatments using dosing regiments that approximates human PK/PD profiles. The prototype instrument will enable real-time data acquisition and treatment modeling.</Course_Objectives>
