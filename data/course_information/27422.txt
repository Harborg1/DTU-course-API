        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>Avanceret forståelse for biosyntese af komplekse naturstoffer som polyketider, non-ribosomale peptider, terpener og stoffer af blandet biosyntetisk oprindelse. Anvendelse af bioinformatisk baserede analyse metoder, som AntiSMASH til annotering af de gener (og klustre), der ligger til grund for de mange typer af enzymkatalyserede reaktioner, der er involveret i biosyntese af naturstoffer. Kurset henvender sig til både biotek og kemi studerende, der gerne vil udvide deres biokemiske kompetencer, og er interesseret i ”naturens organiske kemi”.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Forelæsninger, opgaver, litteratursøgning, gruppearbejde og studenterfremlæggelser</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>Det overordnede formål med dette kursus er at give de studerende et uddybende kendskab til: i) biosyntese af forskellige typer af komplekse små organiske molekyler (sekundære metabolitter = naturstoffer); ii) de forskellige typer af enzymer, som katalyserer biokemiske reaktioner, samt; iii) opbygningen af sekundære metabolit kodende genklustre, og  iv) hvorledes man kan bruge bioinformatiske metoder til at koble viden om gener til kemiske strukturer og omvendt.</Course_Objectives>
        </Txt>
        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>Advanced understanding in biosynthesis of complex natural products, such as polyketides, bioactive ribosomal and non-ribosomal peptides, terpenoids and compounds of mixed biosynthetic origin. Use of bioinformatic tools such as antiSMASH, BLAST, and other tools for annotation of genes, gene clusters, and prediction of the nature of enzymes involved in the biosynthesis of natural products.
Importantly, this course is directed towards both biotech students, as well as students interested in organic chemistry, who want to strengthen their biochemical knowledge.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Lectures, exercises, literature search, group work, and student presentations</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Objectives>The overall aim of the course is to give the students a comprehensive overview of: i) the different types of complex small organic molecules (secondary metabolites = natural products) that microorganisms, algae and plants can biosynthesize; ii) the different types of unique enzymes that catalyzes their biosynthesis; iii) the nature of the underlying gene clusters, and iv) how bioinformatics tools can be used to link genes to chemical structures and vice versa.</Course_Objectives>
        </Txt>
