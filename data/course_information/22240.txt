        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>Kurset er struktureret i 3 forbundne dele som bygger fra teori til praktisk håndtering til selvstændigt design. 
I den første del af kurset bliver studerende introduceret til det teoretiske fundament af præcisions-genteknologi i mammale systemer, med fokus på CRISPR gene editing. Studerende bliver introduceret til nukleaser og precisions editing, inklusiv gene knockout, homology-directed repair, base editing, og prime editing. Kurset vil også adressere DNA-reparations pathways, off-target effekter, midlertidig genregulering og levering af editing systemer til mammale celler med fokus på biotek og medicin.  

I den anden del af kurset får de studerende mulighed for selv at afprøve modificering af cellekulturer med CRISPR i labbet. De studerende udfører co-transfektion for at lave genetiske edits og vil derefter analysere det fænotypiske og genotypiske outcome. Det inkluderer DNA-ekstraktion, PCR-amplicering, flowcytometri og integrering af Sanger-sekventeringsdata til at kvantificere editing-effektiviteten og reparationsraten. 

I den tredje del af kurset, sideløbende med undervisningsmodulerne og laboratoriearbejdet, vil der køre et semesterlangt 'Therapeutic Design Project'. Dette kræver, at de studerende selvstændigt modellerer en genetisk intervention for en defineret monogenetisk sygdom. De studerende skal integrere domænespecifikke machine learning-modeller (f.eks. inDelphi, AlphaFold) for at forudsige udfaldet af DNA-reparation og i proteinstrukturen. 

Efter kurset vil de studerende være rustede til uafhængigt at designe, udføre, og kritisk evaluere CRISPR-baserede genetiske engineeringstrategier i mammale celler, med bro mellem laboratorie-eksperimenter og terapi.</Contents>

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>The course is structured into three interconnected parts that build progressively from theory to hands-on application to autonomous design.
In the first part of the course, students are introduced to the theoretical foundations of genetic engineering in mammalian cells. This covers nuclease systems and precision editing strategies, including gene knockout, homology-directed repair, base editing, and prime editing. Topics also include DNA repair pathways, off-target effects, transient gene regulation, and delivery systems for mammalian cells, with emphasis on their relevance in biotechnology and medicine.
 
In the second part of the course, students gain hands-on experience with a mammalian cell editing workflow. Using a cultured cell model, students perform co-transfection experiments to generate targeted genomic changes and analyse the outcomes at both phenotypic and genotypic levels. This includes genomic DNA extraction, target amplification, flow cytometry, and interpretation of Sanger sequencing data using sequence deconvolution tools to quantify editing efficiency and repair outcomes.
 
In the third part of the course, running in parallel with the taught modules and laboratory work, students undertake a semester-long Therapeutic Design Project. This problem-based learning initiative requires students to autonomously model a genetic intervention for a defined monogenic pathology, integrating domain-specific predictive machine learning models (e.g., inDelphi, AlphaFold) to forecast DNA repair outcomes and protein structural alterations.
 
By the end of the course, students will be equipped to independently design, execute, and critically evaluate CRISPR-based genetic engineering strategies in mammalian cells, bridging the gap between laboratory experimentation and therapeutic application.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>The goal is to provide students with practical and theoretical knowledge and competencies in state-of-the-art mammalian cell engineering. The course integrates advanced CRISPR/Cas modalities, gene editing design and analysis, and phenotypic/genotypic validation to foster autonomous therapeutic problem-solving.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
