        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>Perturbationsteori for differentialligninger, Melnikov-teori, chaotic dynamics, averaging, WKB, geometrisk singulær perturbationsteori, blowup</Contents>
          <Course_Objectives>Differentialligninger er fundamentale i modeleringen af tidsafhængige systemer i ingeniørvidenskaben, og optræder i alt fra kontrolsystemer til fluid dynamik. Det er dog ofte umuligt at løse ligningerne eksakt. Heldigvis kan mange realistiske systemer beskrives som små afvigelser (perturbationer) fra simplere systemer, som man lettere kan løse og forstå. Perturbationsteori er et kraftfuldt værktøj til at approksimere og forstå de mere komplekse systemer udfra de simplere udgaver. Denne teori har været succesfuld i mange grene af den anvendte videnskab. Vi afdækker forskellige udgaver af perturbationsteori, inkluderende averaging, Melnikov teori, WKB approksimation, og (geometrisk) singulær perturbationsteori. Mere bredt, så er målsætningen med kurset at give studerende en teoretisk forståelse (vha dynamisk systemteori) for perturbationsteknikker baseret på udvikling i en lille parameter. Derudover, vil vi demonstrere brugen af perturbationsmetoder på tværs af forskellige felter, inklusiv fysik, mekanik, kemi og biologi.</Course_Objectives>
          <Remark>Kurset kan tages parallelt med 01622.</Remark>

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>Perturbation theory of differential equations, Melnikov theory, chaotic dynamics, averaging, WKB, geometric singular perturbation theory, blowup</Contents>
          <Course_Objectives>Differential equations are fundamental to modeling time-varying systems in engineering, appearing in everything from control systems to fluid dynamics. However, solving them exactly is often challenging or even impossible. Fortunately, many real-world systems are only slight variations—perturbations—of simpler systems that we can analyze more easily. Perturbation theory provides a powerful framework to approximate and understand these more complex systems in terms of their simpler counterparts, offering valuable insights that have been successfully applied across numerous engineering disciplines. We will study various variants of perturbation theory, including averaging, Melnikov theory, WKB approximation, and (geometric) singular perturbation theory. More broadly, the course provides a theoretical foundation (based upon dynamical systems theory) of perturbation techniques that involve expansions in small parameters. In addition, we will demonstrate the application of these perturbation methods across diverse fields, including physics, mechanics, chemistry, and biology.</Course_Objectives>
