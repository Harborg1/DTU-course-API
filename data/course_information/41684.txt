        <Txt Lang="da-DK">
          <Contents>Kurset vil fokuserer på materialesystemerne metaller, keramer og polymerer, og hvordan disse materialer repræsenterer vigtige byggeblokke i mange industrielle produkter, samt hvordan disse er konstrueret. Generelle materialekoncepter der illustrerer proces-mikrostruktur-egenskaber-"performance" vil blive gennemgået.

Kurset dækker følgende emner:
- Struktur og generelle egenskaber af polymerer, keramer og metaller
- Amorfe materialer; struktur, egenskaber og opførsel af amorfe keramer, polymerer og metaller
- Keramer, polymerer og metaller: Mikrostuktur og relaterede egenskaber
- Tekniske metalliske legeringer; systemer med ligevægt og ikke-ligevægt. Mikrostruktur, behandling og egenskaber/"performance" af teknisk relevante legeringer: Stål, aluminium- og titanlegeringer. Generelle styrkeøgende mekanismer i disse systemer.
Nedbrydning af materialer; hvordan vil forskellige materialesystemer (metaller, polymerer og keramer) undergå nedbrydning under brug i rigtige applikationer. Nedbrydning dækker både mekanisk og kemisk nedbrydning.
- Samspillet mellem forskellige materialesystemer vil blive illustreret, for eksempel hvordan keramiske belægninger bruges i vidt omfang til mange metalliske legeringer etc. Herunder også overfladebehandling af materialer som en måde at forhindre nedbrydning på og opnå en levetidsforlængelse. Forskellige overfladebelægninger og overfladebehandlinger vil blive gennemgået (mekanisk og kemisk).
- Samspillet mellem forskellige materialer, materialevalg og hvordan materialer er konstrueret vil blive undersøgt ved at se på faktiske produkter (poster session).</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Forelæsning, opgaver og gruppearbejde i klassen samt mindre hjemmeopgaver. Gruppearbejde for udarbejdelse af en poster.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>The course will focus on the materials systems metals, ceramics and polymers and how these materials represent important building stones for many industrial products and how they are engineered. Generic concepts of materials that showcase process-microstructure-properties-performance will be covered. 

The course covers the following topics:
- Structure and general properties of polymers, ceramics and metals
- Amorphous materials; structure, properties and behavior of amorphous ceramics, polymers and metals
- Ceramics, polymers and metals: microstructure and related properties
- Technological metal alloy systems; equilibrium and non-equilibrium systems. Microstructure, processing and properties/performance of technological relevant alloys:  steels, aluminum and titanium alloys. Generic strengthening methods in such systems. 
- Degradation of materials; how will the different materials systems (metals, polymers and ceramics) suffer from degradation when exposed in real-life applications. Degradation covers both mechanical and chemical properties. 
- The interplay between different materials systems will be elucidated, e.g. ceramic coatings are widely used on many metal alloys etc.  This also entails surface treatment of materials as a means to circumvent degradation and prolong the lifetime. Different surface coatings and surface modifications of materials will be covered (mechanical and chemical).
- The interplay between different materials, choice of materials and how materials are engineered will be investigated by looking at actual products (poster session).</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Lectures, classroom exercises/group work and small home assignments. Group work for a poster assignment.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Literature>W.D. Callister, D.G. Rethwisch, Materials Science and Engineering, SI version, Wiley. Additional relevant material will be available on-line and for free for the students.</Course_Literature>
          <Course_Objectives>The course will enable the students to optimize properties and performance of metals, ceramics and polymers. The course emphasizes the relation between process–microstructure–properties–performance for the technologically important engineering materials: metals, ceramics and polymers.</Course_Objectives>
          <Remark>B.Sc. students in General Engineering are given priority.</Remark>
