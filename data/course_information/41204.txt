        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>The course is coherently comprised of lectures and subsequent exercise sessions. The exercises serve as examples and for practicing analytical and numerical methods. The overall content is covered in the following parts:

1. Loads and reactions by forces and moments.

2. Reactions and bar forces in truss structures.

3. Reactions and section forces in beams and frames.

4. Deformations and deflections of beams and frames.

5. Theory for columns and their dimensioning.

Examples of structural analysis make use of simple numerical programs, while design with optimal use of material is included in connection with the determination of load carrying capacity, deformations and column instability.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Lectures and exercises</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Literature>Steen Krenk &amp; Jan Høgsberg, "Statics and Mechanics of Structures", Springer, 2013.</Course_Literature>
          <Course_Objectives>The goal of the course is to give a basic understanding of how structures work, and provide knowledge of methods for analysis and design of simple structures. The course provides the basis for courses in structural design and more advanced theory of structural elements. Simple numerical tools are introduced to determine reactions, section forces and deformations. 

The main theme of the course is bars and beams and their role in statically determinate truss and frame structures. Central elements of the course are: determination of reactions and internal forces and moments in statically determinate structures for given loads, as well as the differential equation for beams, instability of columns and calculation of deformations by the principle of virtual work.</Course_Objectives>
