          <Contents>Kurset introducerer dig til bæredygtighedsbegrebet og FN's verdensmål og til teknologiens rolle i en fremtidig bæredygtig udvikling. Du bliver introduceret til en metode og vejledning i livscyklus-baseret vurdering af bæredygtighedskonsekvenser af forskningsprojekter, og du anvender metoden på dit eget ph.d.projekt (eller alternativt til et foruddefineret forskningsprojekt) og analyserer hvilke bæredygtighedsimplikationer, resultaterne af dit ph.d.projekt kan få. Derudover vil du blive introduceret til CO₂‑beregning og til konceptet om absolut bæredygtighedsvurdering. Du vil anvende CO₂‑beregning på en foruddefineret case study og supplere den med en absolut bæredygtighedsvurdering (kun for klimaændringer) for at opnå praktisk erfaring og forstå den type fortolkning og beslutningsstøtte, der kan udledes af disse vurderinger.

Kurset introducerer dig også til generel kommunikationsteori og praksis og mere specifikt at kommunikere til ikke-eksperter På kurset bliver du introduceret til teori og redskaber til kommunikation. Via praktiske øvelser og gruppearbejde får du mulighed for at træne dine kommunikationsfærdigheder i løbet af kurset. Når du har gennemført kurset, forventes det, at du giver en præsentation enten på et gymnasium eller til et andet publikum af ikke-eksperter. 

Kurset er på 2,5 ECTS og omfatter 4.5-5 dages forelæsninger, øvelser og gruppearbejde (forventet 30-35 timer). Der er desuden hjemmearbejde både i forbindelse med forberedelse og færdiggørelse af bæredygtighedsvurderingen og formidlingsopgaven (forventet 35-40 timer).

Medmindre andet er aftalt inden kursusstart med kursusansvarlige, er fysisk fremmøde obligatorisk alle kursusdage.</Contents>

          <Course_Objectives>DTU ønsker, at universitetets forskning skal understøtte en bæredygtig udvikling og i særdeleshed bidrage til at nå FN's verdensmål for en bæredygtig udvikling (Sustainable Development Goals, SDGs). Alle ph.d.-studerende på DTU skal derfor opnå en forståelse af bæredygtighed og bæredygtig udvikling, hvilket gør dem i stand til at relatere deres egen forskning til verdensmålene og til at udvikle deres forskning hen imod at bidrage positivt til at indfri dem – både i deres forskning og i deres fremtidige professionelle virke. Som en del af dette læringsmål skal de også have en overordnet praktisk forståelse af CO₂‑beregning og dens forbindelse til planetære grænser. Alle ph.d.-studerende skal derfor træne deres kommunikationsevner og være i stand til at kommunikere om deres forskning i populærvidenskabelig form til ikke-eksperter.</Course_Objectives>

        <Txt Lang="en-GB">
          <Contents>The course introduces you to the concept of sustainability and the United Nation’s Sustainable Development Goals (UN SDGs) and to the role of technology in sustainable development. You are introduced to a methodology and guideline in life cycle based evaluation of the sustainability implications of research projects, and you will apply the methodology to your own PhD project (or, alternatively, to a pre-defined research project) to analyse the potential sustainability implications that the outcomes of your research may have. Moreover, you will be introduced to carbon footprinting and to the concept of absolute sustainability assessment. You will apply carbon footprint to a predefined case study and complement it with an absolute sustainability assessment (for climate change only) to acquire practical experience and grasp the type of interpretation and decision support that can be derived from these assessments.

The course also introduces you to theory and practice of communication in general and to a non-expert audience in particular. During the course you are introduced to theory and tools for communicating, and via individual exercises and group work you get to practice your communication skills during the course. As part of the course, you are expected to prepare and deliver a presentation at either a Danish high school or submit a video of yourself giving a presentation aimed at a high school audience.

The course workload of 2.5 ECTS includes the 5 in-class days of lectures, exercises and group work (expected 30-35 hours) as well as homework related to both the preparation and finalisation of the SDG evaluation report and communication support (expected 35-40 hours).

Unless agreed before course start with course responsibles, onsite attendance is mandatory for all days in the course.</Contents>
          <Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>Introductory lectures and application of presented methods in work on own PhD project and on pre-defined cases individually and in small teams.

Attendance during all scheduled days is mandatory and a requirement for passing the course.</Teaching_And_Learning_Methods>
          <Course_Literature>Sustainability evaluation guideline and selected papers</Course_Literature>
          <Course_Objectives>It is DTU’s ambition that research at the university shall support sustainable development, including contributing to the achievement of the United Nations’ Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). All PhD students at DTU must therefore achieve an understanding of sustainability and sustainable development that enables them to position and develop their research in the context of these goals and contribute positively to the achievement of these goals in their research and future professional work. As part of this objective, they must also have an overall practical understanding of carbon footprinting and its link to planetary boundaries. Moreover, DTU wants its PhD students to be able to reflect on the role of science and research in the public debate. All PhD students must therefore train their communication skills and be able to communicate their research to a non-expert audience in a popular science form.</Course_Objectives>
